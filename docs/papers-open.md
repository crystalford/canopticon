# The McLuhan series: what is still open (2026-11-23)

Not served. The papers' public "Before submission" checklists were resolved
where they could be and removed; this is what is left, and what was done.

## Done on 2026-11-23

- **Every reference** in the seven series papers has a DOI or address where one
  exists, checked against its Crossref or Europe PMC record (about 190 rows,
  matched by first author and year; about 30 wrong matches corrected by hand).
  Each carries a status for what was checked: Text checked, Opened, Abstract
  read, Record checked, Secondary or Cited (the legend is on every page).
- **McLuhan's quotations** were checked word for word against a scan of
  *Understanding Media* (archive.org ETC0624), a scan of *The Medium Is the
  Massage* (2001 printing) and a transcript of the 1969 *Playboy* interview.
  Every one is found except those marked Secondary below.
- **Syncrasis** brought in line with the later papers (Krasis's own item):
  the predictions are now five questions for measurement, "What would sink it"
  is gone, and the unsourced "I don't explain, I explore" (1965) is replaced
  by McLuhan's words to *Playboy* in 1969: "I'm making explorations" and
  "I've never presented such explorations as revealed truth." (A printed
  source for the old line does exist: a 1969 French interview headed
  "Entretien avec McLuhan : 'Je n'explique rien, j'explore'", *Communication
  et langages* 2: 89–100, doi:10.3406/colan.1969.3737, if it is ever wanted.)
- **Unnamed studies named**: Banellis et al. (2026) in *Nature Mental Health*;
  Rapp et al. (2023); Tuip et al. (2025); Winsler et al. (2000, 2007).
- **Specific studies for general claims**: benzodiazepines and auditory evoked
  potentials (Semlitsch, Anderer & Saletu 1995; Rosburg et al. 2004);
  remission from dependence (Lopez-Quintero et al. 2011, beside Heyman 2013);
  the psilocybin–nicotine-patch trial (Johnson et al. 2026, *JAMA Network
  Open*); the Facebook and Instagram deactivation study (Allcott et al. 2026,
  *AEJ: Economic Policy*; the paper had said "about 40% smaller", now the
  published figures).
- **Works cited but missing from the lists** added: Cortese et al. (2018),
  Sonuga-Barke (2003), Ackerman & Goldsmith (2011).
- **Coined terms**: a web search for each exact term (syncrasis,
  heterocrasis, typomora, epanaphony, exocrasis, pharmakrasis, narcocrasis,
  anacrasis) found no prior use. Google Scholar and Google Books cannot be
  searched from the workspace; a two-minute check there is still worth doing.
- Krasis's links to the working drafts now point to the published papers.

## Still open — the author's decisions

- **Readers.** A clinician with ADHD expertise for *Heterocrasis*, and a
  clinician or addiction researcher for *Narcocrasis* §9.
- **Typomora's first-person case**: keep it in the paper or move it to a
  companion essay.
- **Typomora's voice-adoption figures** come from DataReportal/GWI and
  Edison's *Infinite Dial*; the primary surveys behind them were not read.
- **Prior framings to read**: Levinson, *Digital McLuhan* (1999); de
  Kerckhove; Strate — for any earlier "bandwidth" reading of the typed era.
  A hand search of *Explorations in Media Ecology* for earlier links between
  sense ratios and predictive processing.
- **Pharmakrasis**: whether the stimulant, alcohol and opioid cells can be
  upgraded with targeted perceptual studies (the alcohol-myopia literature);
  the page in Leary's *Flashbacks* for the McLuhan lunch.

## Quoted through secondary sources (marked Secondary on the page)

- McLuhan (1960), NAEB *Report on Project in Understanding New Media*:
  "It is the ratio among our senses which is violently disturbed by media
  technology" (via Friesen, *Enculturation*, citing p. 9).
- McLuhan (1967), "The Invisible Environment", *Perspecta* 11
  (doi:10.2307/1566945): "The really total and saturating environments are
  invisible." The article is confirmed; the wording is not.
- *War and Peace in the Global Village* (1968), the fish and water: the
  Internet Archive's copies are lending-only. The paper calls it "widely
  attributed", which is accurate.
- Cavell (2002) on Jonas; Miller (1971) through Kermode; the Nietzsche letter.
- *The Gutenberg Galaxy* (1962) and *Laws of Media* are cited, not reopened.
