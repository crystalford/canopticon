# The Blind Spot — Companion Note: The Unrealized-Gains Channel

> **Caveat first.** Everything here is a CANOPTICON construction. The
> unrealized-gains channel is the single largest and the single least directly
> measured piece of the Invisibility Index. Treat it as the instrument's
> central argument, not its most solid number.

## Why the largest weight (55%)?

Because the failure is structural, not statistical. An asset that appreciates but is never sold generates no realized income. Income-based methods — including the capitalization method that produces the **highest** official estimate — capitalize income flows, so an asset producing no income contributes nothing to capitalize. The wealth is real, growing, and borrowable against, but invisible to the measurement.

## The buy–borrow–die mechanism

Fox-Liscow's framing:

1. **Buy** appreciating assets.
2. **Borrow** against them to fund consumption — a loan is not income.
3. **Die** and pass them with a stepped-up basis.

At no point does the gain become taxable income; at no point does an income-based wealth estimate see it. This is why the channel gets the largest share and is flagged the loudest: it is where measurement and reality diverge most, and where the instrument's argument is strongest and least verifiable.

## How it enters the Index

The unrealized-gains channel takes 55% of the total measured gap (see [`capitalization-factors.md`](./capitalization-factors.md)). The remaining gap is split across private equity (33%, residual), offshore wealth (8%), and survey non-response (4%). Every one of these is a stated assumption, not a measurement — the total gap is what's measured.
