"The housing crisis is a supply problem."
The propagation of a framing through Canadian public discourse. When did this specific framing first appear, who introduced it, who amplified it, who funds the amplifiers, and what is their material interest in this framing prevailing. Not conspiracy thinking — structural analysis of how ideas move through political ecosystems.

First sustained appearance

2017
CMHC research note, "Housing Supply and Affordability" — November 2017
The framing of the affordability problem as fundamentally one of insufficient supply enters Canadian policy discourse in coherent form here. Earlier statements existed but in fragmented form across multiple documents. The 2017 note consolidates the framing and provides the citation infrastructure used by subsequent commentators.

FUNDERS' MATERIAL INTEREST

The material interest of the funder coalition in the supply-framing prevailing is direct and large.

Big Five banks: mortgage origination is the central credit-creation activity in Canadian banking. The structural framing names mortgage securitization as a problem; the supply framing leaves mortgage origination unexamined. Banks' interest is in the supply framing winning.

Developer industry: the supply framing positions developers as the solution. The structural framing positions them as one element among many. Industry interest is in the supply framing winning.

Real estate brokerage: commission income depends on transaction volume, which depends on continued participation of investor-buyers. The structural framing threatens that demand source; the supply framing leaves it untouched.

Existing homeowners (the median Canadian household): approximately two-thirds of household wealth in Canada is in real estate. The supply framing is consistent with continued price appreciation; the structural framing aims at price moderation. Median-household interest is in the supply framing winning.

Aggregate effect: every major institutional actor in Canadian housing discourse has material interest in the supply framing prevailing. The structural framing's institutional support is concentrated in heterodox academic circles whose platform reach is small. The pattern is not conspiracy — it is the predictable result of each actor responding to its own incentives.
