Why does the Canadian housing crisis persist despite two decades of policy attention?

The full six-layer argument tree for a contested public question. Most public debate operates at Layer 1 — the surface — while the real disagreement usually lives at Layer 4 (empirical) or Layer 5 (values), and the structural reason it never resolves is at Layer 6.
1
LAYER 1 · SURFACE

What people actually argue about

The bumper-sticker level. What's debated on Twitter, in headlines, in question period.

SUPPLY-SIDE
  • "We need to build more housing."
  • "Cut red tape and zoning."
  • "Increased immigration produces this crisis."
  • "Foreign buyers are the problem."
STRUCTURAL-SIDE
  • "We've always built; it doesn't help affordability."
  • "Building more luxury condos doesn't house people."
  • "Foreign-buyer blame is xenophobic deflection."
  • "The crisis is engineered, not accidental."
2
LAYER 2 · EVIDENCE

What each side cites

The studies and statistics each side relies on. Real evidence, but selective.

SUPPLY-SIDE EVIDENCE
CMHC supply-gap analyses (2022–2024). Per-capita housing-starts comparisons. Zoning-permit time-to-issue data from BC and Ontario. Vacant-land inventories.
STRUCTURAL-SIDE EVIDENCE
BoC credit-creation data showing mortgage-debt growth outpacing GDP since 2002. Tax-treatment comparisons. Concentration metrics on Big Five mortgage origination. CPPIB asset allocation shifts.
3
LAYER 3 · DEPENDENCIES

What has to be true for each side's surface claim to hold

The premises beneath the visible claims. Often unstated.

SUPPLY-SIDE DEPENDS ON
That housing-as-shelter is the marginal demand driving prices. That supply elasticity is the binding constraint. That investor demand is bounded by cap rates. That the policy lever is municipal/provincial.
STRUCTURAL-SIDE DEPENDS ON
That housing-as-investment-asset is the marginal demand driving prices. That credit creation is the binding constraint. That tax design and securitization architecture set demand. That the policy lever is federal monetary and fiscal.
4
LAYER 4 · EMPIRICAL DISPUTES

Where facts are actually contested — resolvable in principle

The specific empirical questions where the evidence has not been assembled. These could be settled with research.

Is the marginal buyer in Canadian urban housing markets a shelter-seeker or an investor?
SUPPLY: shelter-seeker; price is a supply-demand artifact. STRUCTURAL: investor; price tracks credit availability and asset returns more than shelter demand.
How much of mortgage-debt growth since 2002 has been absorbed by existing-property revaluation versus new construction?
SUPPLY: minimally relevant. STRUCTURAL: central to the diagnosis; the data exists but is not assembled in publicly-cited form.
What is the empirical elasticity of urban housing prices to municipal zoning reforms over a 5-year window?
SUPPLY: high; reforms produce affordability improvement. STRUCTURAL: low; reforms produce supply but not price correction.
5
LAYER 5 · VALUES DISPUTES

Where priors differ — evidence alone cannot resolve

Genuine value-laden questions. These are not factual disputes in disguise.

Should existing homeowners' wealth be protected, or should the policy goal be reducing housing as a class of investment?
SUPPLY: protect homeowner equity; politically and economically necessary. STRUCTURAL: shrink the asset class; current homeowner wealth is a legacy effect of the system, not its purpose.
Is intergenerational transfer of housing wealth legitimate or distortionary?
Implicit acceptance in supply-side framing; explicit critique in structural framing.
6
LAYER 6 · META

Why this argument keeps recurring without resolution

The structural reason for the stalemate.

WHY THIS DEBATE PERSISTS

The supply-side framing is structurally favored by every major institutional actor in Canadian housing discourse: CMHC's mandate is supply-oriented; the construction industry funds research aligned with supply analysis; the Big Five banks have material interest in preserving mortgage origination as the central credit-creation activity; existing homeowners (the median household and most political-class members) have material interest in continued asset appreciation.

The structural framing has institutional support primarily in heterodox economics circles and post-Keynesian academic work, neither of which has the platform reach of CMHC research or bank-funded think tanks.

Result: the empirical questions at Layer 4 — which could in principle be resolved by research — are not being resolved because the institutions that would fund and disseminate the research have material interest in the supply-side framing prevailing.

This is not a conspiracy. It is structural. Each actor responds to its own incentives. The aggregate effect is an argument that cannot resolve at the level it's being conducted.

Intervention by leverage level

SYMPTOM LEVEL · LOWEST LEVERAGE

  • Build more shelter beds, expand emergency housing supports.
  • Down-payment assistance programs, first-time-buyer incentives.
  • Rent subsidies to households below income threshold.

MECHANISM LEVEL · MEDIUM LEVERAGE

  • Vacancy taxes and foreign-buyer bans.
  • Zoning reforms accelerating municipal approvals.
  • Stress tests on mortgage qualification.
  • Public housing construction at scale.

STRUCTURAL LEVEL · HIGHEST LEVERAGE

  • Reform of mortgage-securitization architecture (CMHC's role in credit creation).
  • Capital-gains tax treatment of primary and secondary residences.
  • Bank Act amendments altering Big Five mortgage-origination concentration.
  • Land-value taxation as primary municipal revenue source.
  • Re-classification of housing as constitutional infrastructure rather than asset class.
