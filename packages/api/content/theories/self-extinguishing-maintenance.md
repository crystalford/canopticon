Some problems stay fixed only while you keep treating them. Two teeth that do not quite touch trap food; cleaning the gap keeps the gum healthy, and within a week it looks fine, so the cleaning stops and the gum becomes sore again. The same pattern appears with skin that stays soft only while moisturised, with toes pressed too tightly together, and with many medicines. Self-extinguishing maintenance is the name for the whole cycle. The research behind it found that the pieces were already studied but the cycle was not: <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Summary">No source names the whole cycle across conditions, and none treats structural anatomy as the cause that never goes away.</q>

## Definition

<q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="G">a maintenance behavior that, by succeeding, removes the symptom that cues and reinforces it, so that the successful state is misread as cure and the behavior stops, while an unchanged underlying cause guarantees relapse.</q>

The belief that drives the stopping has its own name, the **remission-as-cure error**: <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="G">"remission-as-cure error" (reading a maintained state as a resolved one)</q>.

The theory has four parts, in order: a symptom that prompts and rewards a routine; a routine that succeeds; a success that is read as a cure; and a cause that was never changed, which brings the symptom back.

## Mechanism

The symptom does two jobs. It is the reminder to act, and its relief is the reward for acting. Once the routine works, both are gone. Behaviour analysts describe the person at that point as one who <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="B">would no longer be motivated to engage in the behavior.</q> As the research puts it, <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Details">a routine cued by a symptom loses its trigger when the symptom goes</q>.

The evidence that the routine matters also disappears while it works: <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Details">While the routine runs, you never see what would happen without it, so the healthy state gets credited to "my body healed" rather than "my routine is holding it."</q> The standard account of why people keep up a behaviour makes the problem worse rather than better. It holds that continuing depends on <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="A">perceived satisfaction with received outcomes</q>. For a maintained condition, the satisfying outcome is the one that ends the routine.

The misreading is understandable. <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="B">The misreading is not stupidity. It is a reasonable generalization from most of one's experience, applied to the minority of conditions where it fails.</q>

## Evidence

The stopping is measured across very different conditions. Across surveys in 13 countries, <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Key findings">the main patient-reported reason for discontinuation was feeling better (46.6%)</q>. For blood-pressure drugs, <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Key findings">about half of the patients who were prescribed an antihypertensive drug had stopped taking it within one year.</q> Among adults hospitalised for asthma, 53% <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Key findings">believed they only had asthma when they were having symptoms.</q>

The relapse is measured too. After long-term moisturiser use stops, dryness <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Details">"reverted to baseline levels after 6 days" by objective assessment</q>. Uncleaned gums <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Details">developed gingivitis within about 9–21 days</q>. Over the long run, one meta-analysis <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Details">reported a pooled tooth-loss risk ratio of 0.56 for regular versus erratic compliers</q>.

The belief that predicts continuing is perceived need: <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Key findings">higher adherence went with stronger beliefs that the treatment is personally necessary (OR 1.742)</q>. Where a field has built the maintenance-forever model into practice, it works. In eczema, a meta-analysis found <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Details">proactive fluticasone (RR 0.46) and tacrolimus (RR 0.78) reduced flare risk compared with vehicle</q>.

## Measures

Four quantities make the theory checkable for any routine.

1. **Standing cause.** Is the cause transient or persistent? The research's rule: <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Recommendations">Transient causes (injury, infection, one-off exposure) → heal and done. Persistent causes (anatomy, spacing, genetics, chronic physiology) → maintenance until the structure changes.</q>
2. **Recurrence count.** How many times has the symptom returned at the same site with the same trigger? <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Recommendations">Two recurrences at the same site with the same trigger is strong evidence of a standing cause.</q>
3. **Relapse latency.** How long after stopping does the symptom return? For skin it is days; for gums, weeks.
4. **Cue source.** Is the routine prompted by the symptom, or by a fixed context that does not disappear when things go well?

A routine is at risk when the cause is standing, the cue source is the symptom, and the relapse latency is long enough for the person to stop noticing the link.

## Protocol

1. **Name the cause.** Decide whether it is transient or persistent, using the recurrence count if unsure.
2. **Move the cue.** <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Recommendations">Tie the routine to a fixed context: after the shower for the toes, after each meal for impaction sites.</q>
3. **Understand the mechanism.** A rule followed only because someone said so does not survive success: <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Details">A child told "floss every day" without the mechanism learns pliance. Once parental enforcement is gone, the behavior has neither a social nor an experienced basis, so it falls apart.</q>
4. **Remove the cause where possible.** <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Recommendations">Where the cause can be removed, removal beats lifelong maintenance.</q>

## Scope

The theory applies when the cause persists: anatomy, spacing, genetics, chronic physiology. It does not apply when the cause ends. Stopping when you feel better is then the right decision, and the research says so about its own antibiotic figure: <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Caveats">stopping antibiotics early is not always wrong.</q>

The same structure appears beyond personal care. Public health describes it for whole populations: <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="A">the success of prevention is invisible, lacks drama, often requires persistent behavior change, and may be long delayed.</q> Software maintenance, equipment upkeep and organisational safeguards are plausible further cases. They have not been studied under this name.

## Counter-evidence

The parts of the theory are measured; the whole loop is not. The research states this plainly: <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Caveats">The reinforcement framing, the cue-loss framing, the proposed name, and the McLuhan reading are this report's synthesis. They are consistent with established principles but not tested as such.</q>

Some of the supporting evidence is weak. For interdental cleaning, the research describes the evidence as <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Details">low-certainty</q>. People also differ in how fast they relapse: <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="Details">Later work identified "fast," "slow," and "low" responder phenotypes.</q> A slow responder may stop and see nothing for a long time, which weakens the link the person would need to learn from.

No study has yet followed people with a known standing cause, recorded what cues their routine, and timed both the stopping and the return of the symptom.
