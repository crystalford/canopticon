# The Medium Becomes the Ground: McLuhan's Media Theory and the Allostatic Model of Addiction (Rebuilt Research Report)
Research document · Canopticon · received 2026-10-04 · condensed from chat; every passage below is verbatim from the report

## Opening
The bridge you remember is real and largely unbuilt. No source I found maps McLuhan onto Koob and Le Moal's allostatic model at the level of mechanism. The strongest and most defensible link is not figure/ground on its own. It is McLuhan's Selye-derived theory of autoamputation and counter-irritation in Understanding Media chapter 4, which shares a physiological ancestor with allostasis.

## TL;DR
Work linking McLuhan to drugs and addiction exists only at the level of discourse and metaphor. The nearest example is Hartogsohn & Vudka (2023, Transcultural Psychiatry), which is about smartphone "addiction" and McLuhan's "Notes on Burroughs."
McLuhan and Koob are cousins, not ancestor and descendant.
In the allostatic model, the thing that becomes the unperceived "ground" is not the drug. It is the drug's opponent process: the anti-reward, stress-surfeit baseline. The drug stays a hyper-salient figure.

## Part 1
Media ecology has absorbed Selye but stopped at homeostasis. Addiction neuroscience moved from homeostasis to allostasis (Koob & Le Moal 1997, 2001) without ever looking back at McLuhan. Connecting the two halves is the contribution.

## Part 2 — McLuhan, Understanding Media (1964)
"Physiologically there are abundant reasons for an extension of ourselves involving us in a state of numbness. Medical researchers like Hans Selye and Adolphe Jonas hold that all extensions of ourselves, in sickness or in health, are attempts to maintain equilibrium."
"In the physical stress of superstimulation of various kinds, the central nervous system acts to protect itself by a strategy of amputation or isolation of the offending organ, sense, or function."
"As counter-irritant, the image produces a generalized numbness or shock that declines recognition. Self-amputation forbids self-recognition."
"Whereas pleasure is a counter-irritant (e.g., sports, entertainment, and alcohol), comfort is the removal of irritants. Both pleasure and comfort are strategies of equilibrium for the central nervous system."
"Shock induces a generalized numbness or an increased threshold to all types of perception."
It continues: "the counter-irritant usually proves a greater plague than the initial irritant, like a drug habit."
In late-stage addiction the drug becomes "comfort" by removing an irritant the drug itself produced.

## The Selye lineage
The fair claim is that McLuhan gave a proto-allostatic phenomenology of extension, built on the same Selyean root that allostasis later revised. Saying he "discovered allostasis" would overclaim.

## Part 3 — the allostatic model
Koob & Le Moal (1997, Science 278:52–58) presented addiction as "a cycle of spiraling dysregulation of brain reward systems that progressively increases, resulting in compulsive drug use and loss of control."
"Allostasis is defined here as the process of achieving stability through change, and allostatic state is defined as a state of chronic deviation of the regulatory system from its normal operating (homeostatic) level."
"All drugs of abuse produce elevations in brain reward thresholds during acute withdrawal" (Koob & Le Moal 2008).
Under extended access, "the progressive elevation in reward thresholds was associated with a dramatic escalation in cocaine consumption" (Koob 2013).
his analysis of four large US surveys found that among people who had ever met dependence criteria, "between 76 per cent and 83 per cent were in remission," mostly without treatment
The figure/ground reading actually fits choice models well: choices made from within an unperceived ground are biased choices.

## Part 4 — constitutive versus modificatory
"A distinct identity gain pathway emerged for socially isolated individuals, who described the onset of their addiction in terms of a new valued social identity."
"Participants on the identity loss pathway aimed to renew their pre-addiction identities after treatment while those on the identity gain pathway aimed to build aspirational new identities involving study, work, or family roles."
In McLuhan's terms, for the constitutive user the drug was never a figure that later became ground. It was the first ground the self was ever figured against.

## Part 5 — caveats
Much of the evidence comes from rodent ICSS and extended-access paradigms. Human data on reward thresholds are indirect.
McLuhan's "threshold" refers to perception, while Koob's ICSS threshold is an electrophysiological reward measure. The overlap in vocabulary supports structural correspondence, not identity.

## Recommendations
In Koob's model what becomes baseline is the opponent process, not the drug. The drug stays hyper-figural through incentive salience, much as McLuhan's "content" is "the juicy piece of meat carried by the burglar to distract the watchdog of the mind." This sharpens your thesis and protects it from the obvious objection that addicts perceive the drug all too clearly.
Abstinence briefly turns the ground into figure, which predicts a window of improved insight in early abstinence.
That is a 1964 statement of the spiralling distress model, written by a media theorist reading Selye.
