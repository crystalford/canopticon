# Time Blindness, Task-Hopping, and Why DoorDash Feels Easy: An Evidence-Based Look at Your Pattern
Research document · Canopticon · received 2026-10-01 · standalone · personal · kind: personal-pattern inquiry
---

"Time blindness" is Russell Barkley's plain-language label for a weak, unreliable internal sense of time and of the future. It's linked to ADHD and has decent support from meta-analyses. Your own twist, *over*estimating how long things take, fits the research better than you might expect. Even so, this pattern doesn't prove ADHD.

## TL;DR

- **"Time blindness" is a popular term, not a diagnosis.** Large meta-analyses find that people with ADHD judge time intervals less accurately and less consistently. The best-known pooled result is Metcalfe et al. (2024), with Hedges' g = 0.688 across 824 effect sizes. Those lab studies time *seconds*, not 30-minute routines.
- **The task-hopping and the DoorDash contrast are the same story.** Deliveries give you everything a weak internal executive system lacks: one visible next action, a countdown, GPS prompts, instant feedback and pay, and a clear start and finish. Barkley calls this needing help "at the point of performance."
- **The "other blindness" you couldn't name is most likely "object permanence"** (out of sight, out of mind), with "future blindness" a close second. Start with a two-week time-logging experiment.

## Key Findings

1. Barkley's 1997 theory: ADHD involves "a form of temporal myopia or time blindness" in which behaviour is "more controlled by the temporal now than by internally represented information pertaining to past and future."
2. Marx et al.'s 2022 meta-analysis of 55 studies: people with ADHD "overestimated stimulus durations in the time estimation paradigm (ie, reported a larger number of seconds elapsed)," which the authors describe as "indicative of an accelerated internal clock."
3. The bigger issue is *inconsistency*. Zheng et al. (2022): less accurate (g > 0.40) and especially less *precise* (g = 0.66).
4. Barkley describes ADHD as "a disorder of doing what you know, not knowing what to do." Delivery apps are an almost perfect point-of-performance prosthetic.
5. Weaker popular explanations: the "interest-based nervous system" (Dodson) is a clinical observation; the simple "low dopamine" story is contested (MacDonald, Kleppe, Szigetvari and Haavik 2024: "limited evidence for a hypo-dopaminergic state per se as a key component of ADHD"); the Zeigarnik effect largely failed to replicate in a 2025 meta-analysis.

## Details

### 1. What "time blindness" means in the research
Meta-analyses: Noreika, Falter & Rubia (2013); Hart et al. (2012) fMRI; Zheng et al. (2022, 27 studies); Marx, Cortese, Koelch & Hacker (2022, 55 studies); Metcalfe, McFeaters & Voyer (2024: 824 effect sizes, 129 samples, 4,692 people with ADHD, g = 0.688). Walg et al. (2017) on the faster internal clock. Jackson & MacKillop's 2016 meta-analysis: d = 0.43 for preferring smaller-sooner rewards. Mette's 2023 review: "the number of studies on time perception in adult ADHD is very scarce." Barkley's 2001 young-adult study: larger estimates "became nonsignificant after controlling for IQ."

### 2. Your twist: over-estimating instead of under-estimating
A fast internal clock. Memory-based estimates never checked (Roy, Christenfeld & McKenzie 2005; correction improves prediction, Roy, Mitten & Christenfeld 2008). Effort and dread inflate time ("increased delay aversion"). Time padding as anxious overcompensation. Inconsistent calibration.

### 3. The "flip the script" pattern: priority-hijacking
Inhibition and goal maintenance. Set-shifting captured by distraction. Delay aversion (Sonuga-Barke). The interest-based nervous system (a heuristic). Structured procrastination (Perry). Open loops: the Zeigarnik effect didn't hold (Ghibellini and Meier 2025); the Ovsiankina effect did — people resume interrupted tasks about 67% of the time. Working memory and "out of sight, out of mind." Decision fatigue (ego depletion replication problems).

### 4. Why DoorDash is easy and your own life isn't
What's next: app shows one order vs you decide over and over. Order: queue set vs everything competes. Time: ETA vs "later." Prompts: GPS vs memory. Feedback: immediate vs invisible. Start/stop: clear vs fuzzy. Hijack resistance: detours cost money vs cost nothing. The app is externalising executive function. **You already have a working template. The skill isn't missing. The scaffolding is.**

### 5. The "other blindness": ranked candidates
1. "Object permanence" (working-memory limits). 2. "Future blindness" / temporal myopia. 3. "Task blindness" / hidden sub-steps. 4. Inattentional blindness. 5. Functional fixedness / Einstellung — links to the pallet-wrap example. 6. Interoception (mixed: Bruton et al. 2025). 7. Alexithymia (Kiraz et al. 2021: 41.5% in the ADHD group). 8. Change blindness. 9. Aphantasia. 10. "Mind blindness." 11. "Habit blindness."

### 6. Connecting back to the pallet wrap
Habit and automaticity; Einstellung (Luchins 1942; Vallée-Tourangeau 2011); time myopia; friction. Where not to over-claim: sticking with a familiar method is universal.

## Recommendations
Two-week experiment: guess / actual / ratio, starting with the morning routine; compute a personal correction factor; a "real durations" cheat sheet. Externalise time: visual timers, analog clocks, anchor alarms, backward planning. Turn personal tasks into delivery-style orders: one order at a time; pickup → drop-off; route for the day; accept the next order only after drop-off; immediate "payout." A parking lot for detours plus if-then plans (Gawrilow & Gollwitzer 2008: children with ADHD improved inhibition "to the same level observed in children without ADHD"; Toli et al. 2016). Reduce friction and add cues. Body doubling (Eagle, Baltaxe-Admony & Ringland 2024; "promising trends for unmedicated ADHD participants"). Sleep (CADDRA: sleep apnea can mimic ADHD). CBT for adult ADHD (Safren et al. 2010 *JAMA*). Coaching.

## Getting a proper assessment in Ontario/Canada
Family doctor (OHIP); psychiatrist by referral (often 6–18 months); private psychologist (about $2,000–$4,000+); private/virtual clinics ($300–$800). CADDRA 4.1 (2021): "A careful and thorough assessment reduces the risk of a false diagnosis of ADHD." ASRS-v1.1 Part A: 68.7% sensitivity, 99.5% specificity in a community sample (Kessler 2005); in adults with major depression a positive result was correct only 21.4% of the time. Song et al. 2021: "the prevalence of persistent adult ADHD was 2.58% and that of symptomatic adult ADHD was 6.76%."

## Caveats
No diagnosis is implied. Ranked alternative explanations: executive-function differences; never measuring tasks; anxiety-driven padding; sleep disruption; low mood; stress and overload; medical contributors. The lab-to-life gap. Popular concepts vary in evidence. Strategies help whether or not you have ADHD.
