# Ancaster Pass 11: The Indie SaaS Advertiser Side — Buyer Reality, Tools, Pricing and the Cascade

Indie founders will not pay Ancaster to track affiliates, because tracking is already a $19–$49/month commodity and free inside several merchant-of-record platforms. Some of them will pay a performance fee when Ancaster brings them creators who actually promote. So give the program software away and charge only on revenue from partners Ancaster sources.

## TL;DR

- **Don't sell tracking; sell activated creators.** Rewardful, Tolt, FirstPromoter, Refgrow, Affonso and PromoteKit all charge a flat subscription with 0% transaction fees, most for $19–$99/month. Lemon Squeezy, Gumroad, Whop and Creem bundle affiliates into checkout, and Lemon Squeezy, Gumroad and Whop also give access to a shared affiliate pool. The piece nobody sells cheaply is recruitment. Dub puts its Partner Network and Marketplace listing on the Enterprise tier only, and PartnerStack's marketplace sits behind estimated four-to-five-figure annual contracts.
- **Recommended packaging:** make the full in-house program free (links, Stripe promo codes, Referral Receipts, statements, and paying partners yourself). Charge a take rate only on commissions earned by Ancaster-sourced creators, plus a booking fee on flat-fee creator placements. Ad serving stays a small flat add-on. This positions Ancaster *above* bundled MoR affiliate features instead of competing with them.
- **Cascade:** start with the creators and seed them with Chris's own offers, not with advertisers. Merchants' core pain is finding and activating partners, so an empty network is worthless to them. Chris's products give developer/AI creators real, high-converting offers from day one. Then recruit the first 20 indie advertisers from founders who already publicly want affiliates (Indie Hackers threads, Polar/Creem/Lemon Squeezy sellers, EthicalAds/Carbon buyers). Kill the advertiser side if, after 30 days, fewer than 3 creators produce content for Chris's offers or no outside founder agrees to the sourced-partner fee.

## Key Findings

1. **Bootstrapped founders prefer channels they control and distrust paid ads.** In MicroConf's 2024 State of Independent SaaS survey (469 analysed founders, data collected late 2023), SEO and word of mouth ranked as the highest-impact marketing activities. 29% of founders running paid ads either wait 7+ months for a return or don't know if the ads work at all. A Freemius summary gives 57% for what appears to be the same measure; the two figures conflict, and only the 29% comes with a page reference.\[1\]\[2\]\[3\]
2. **No independent survey measures how many indie SaaS run affiliate programs.** MicroConf, Indie Hackers, Baremetrics, ChartMogul and Stripe publish no prevalence figure. Everything available is vendor data or anecdote.
3. **Small affiliate programs are small.** In Rewardful's analysis of 250 of its *top* programs, the 40.8% with under $100k in annual affiliate revenue averaged 393 referred conversions a year. Programs under one year old averaged $120k in annual sales versus $330k for 3–4-year-old programs, though Rewardful itself cautions that the groups are small (18 vs 43 programs) and "survivor bias may influence these figures." A saas.group partnerships expert quoted in the report says new programs need "6 to 12 months of 'ramp up time'" and that "many affiliates that we recruit won't start selling until a few months later."
4. **Tracking is commoditized; recruitment is gated.** Dub Partners is $90/month (Business: $2.5K partner payouts/month, 500 partners, 5% payout fee) or $300/month (Advanced: $15K payouts, 5% fee). Only Enterprise (custom pricing, 3% fee) gets "Partner Network access" and a "Program Marketplace listing."\[4\]
5. **Bundled merchant-of-record affiliates are a real threat to *tracking*, but a weak one to *recruitment*.** Lemon Squeezy charges merchants a 3% fee per affiliate-referred order and handles payouts. Gumroad, Whop and Creem bundle affiliates too. Stripe, Paddle and Polar have no native affiliate program, and Polar deliberately partners with Affonso instead.\[5\]\[6\]\[7\]\[8\]
6. **Developer ad networks have high floors for an indie.** EthicalAds has a $1,000 minimum buy (CPM).\[9\]\[10\] Carbon recommends $5,000–$10,000/month for 60–90 days.\[11\] beehiiv's Ad Network is sales-led with $15k minimums.\[12\] ChatGPT Ads went fully self-serve with no minimum on May 5, 2026, the one new channel with a low entry point.\[13\]\[14\]\[15\]
7. **Hybrid "flat fee plus performance" precedents exist but are fragmented.** Dub has program bounties (including social-metrics bounties).\[4\] SparkLoop does pay-per-engaged-subscriber.\[16\]\[17\] Paved and Passionfroot sell flat-fee placements.\[18\]\[19\] No product found combines a booked creator placement with an attributed commission on the same offer, customer and receipt.

## 1. The Indie SaaS Founder as a Buyer

**Where they congregate.** The query's list (Indie Hackers, X build-in-public, r/SaaS, r/SideProject, MicroConf, Product Hunt, Show HN, Discords) matches where this pass found activity. Indie Hackers has a long run of "affiliate program for SaaS" threads, in which founders both ask for affiliates and sell affiliate directories (e.g., a founder building "afftable.com" pitching a SaaS affiliate listing).\[20\] MicroConf runs deliberately small events (200–250 founders).\[1\] The newer merchant-of-record crowd (Polar, Creem, Lemon Squeezy) is a distinct cluster: Creem markets directly to Lovable/Replit/Bolt builders.\[21\] This pass did not measure the relative size or engagement of each community; treat the list as qualitative.

**Budgets and channels.** No accessible source gives indie SaaS marketing budgets. MicroConf's public pages say "you don't need an ad budget to grow at this stage" and name SEO and word of mouth as top channels.\[1\] About half of MicroConf's independent SaaS respondents are solo-founded (Freemius separately reports 45.7% of its SaaS makers are solo). Freemius's December 2025 post "AI-Driven, Founder-Led: The 2025 State of Micro-SaaS," citing MicroConf, says 47% found "integrations, partnerships, communities, and forums became a more dependable source of growth". It introduces that figure with its own framing ("many founders shifted toward channels they could understand and control"), and it can't be checked against the report. What this means for Ancaster: the buyer is a solo founder with little or no paid budget who dislikes spending before seeing results. That rules out subscription-before-value pricing.

**What they say works and doesn't for affiliates (anecdote, Indie Hackers):**
- "We knew they didn't just magically work. You have to invest time and have affiliates ready to go." (Versoly)\[22\]
- "Affiliate programs would not be my go-to approach for marketing a SaaS application unless you have relationships with influencers or BD folks." A commenter in the same thread recommended joint webinars with partner audiences instead.\[23\]
- One founder added "$3k in revenue" after cold-outreaching "tons of sales agencies." Having a program was not enough; outreach was the work.\[24\]
- One Tolt reviewer reports "around 20% of my revenue comes through Tolt affiliate links." This is a single, vendor-solicited review.\[25\]

**How many run programs, and results.** No independent prevalence data exists (see Key Finding 2). Vendor data points:
- Rewardful's 250-program analysis is described above. The data covers referred-customer activity, not affiliate counts.\[26\]
- Rewardful case material says beehiiv's affiliate program drives 12–14% of MRR and affiliates drive 22% of Pallyy's MRR.\[27\]
- A Rewardful case study (AddSearch) reports ~100 active affiliates after moving off PartnerStack's $18,000/year.\[28\]
- GoAffPro, a vendor, states a benchmark that 10–20% of affiliates should be "genuinely active."\[29\]
- Earlier passes found 75% of brands have ≤100 sale-active publishers (PMA 2024) and 60% of marketers say keeping affiliates active is their biggest challenge (Modash 2026).

**Evidence quality flag:** Every results figure in this section except MicroConf's comes from a vendor that sells affiliate software. The 250 programs are Rewardful's *top* programs, so they overstate typical results.\[26\] The typical indie program probably has a handful of affiliates and a small single-digit share of revenue, but no dataset proves this.

**Payment-platform native features:**
- **Lemon Squeezy:** built-in affiliates.
  - Fee: the current docs say 3% per affiliate-referred order on top of platform fees; the 2023 launch blog said 2%. Lemon Squeezy's own Fees doc confirms it also takes "+2% for affiliate payouts (affiliates)" from each affiliate's commission.
  - Payouts: per Lemon Squeezy's Affiliate Payouts doc, payouts are created on the 1st and 15th of each month and paid on the 14th and 28th, after commissions are held for 30 days; $10 minimum. It vets affiliate applicants.
  - Distribution: merchants can list in an Affiliate Hub "in front of thousands of affiliates."\[43\]
  - Caveat: Stripe owns Lemon Squeezy, and a competitor notes nobody outside Stripe can say what the hub becomes if stores move to Stripe's rails.\[44\]
- **Gumroad:** per-product affiliate rates of 1–75%, plus a marketplace-wide "Gumroad Affiliates" program at a fixed 10%. Fees are split proportionally with the affiliate. Gumroad pays out and claws back refunds automatically.\[45\]\[46\]
- **Whop:** every whop has affiliates on by default at 30%. There is an Affiliate Marketplace that shows EPC and conversion rates, and a 30-day commission hold. Whop claims 30,000+ earning affiliates.\[47\]\[48\]
- **Creem:** a merchant of record at 3.9% + 40¢ with built-in affiliate tracking, commission splits and payouts. A competitor describes the Affiliate Hub as beta. Creem also integrates Affonso.\[49\]\[50\]\[51\]
- **Polar:** no native affiliates. It chose to "find a great partner… vs. spread ourselves thin" and integrated Affonso.\[8\]
- **Stripe and Paddle:** no native merchant affiliate program. Stripe offers building blocks (promotion codes, Checkout metadata, Connect).\[5\]\[6\]\[52\] This comes from consistent third-party sources, not Stripe or Paddle docs.

**Where the gap is.** Tracking, codes and payouts cost $19–$49 or are free, so there is no gap there. The real gaps:
1. **Recruitment at indie prices.** Dub gates its network to Enterprise; PartnerStack's marketplace needs a sales contract.
2. **Activation.** No tool gives affiliates a ready-to-publish, disclosed, converting page. Ancaster's Interstitial Standard offer pages do exactly this.
3. **Proof the creator accepts.** The Referral Receipt covers this, and Dub's criticism of fragmented affiliate logins shows creators want one place.
4. **Cross-rail neutrality.** A program that survives moving between Stripe, Polar, Creem and Lemon Squeezy.

**Threat assessment of bundled MoR affiliates: high for tracking, moderate for recruitment, low for activation.** A founder on Creem or Lemon Squeezy has zero reason to pay Ancaster for links and commissions. Lemon Squeezy and Whop marketplaces do offer recruitment, but it is generic and not curated to developer/AI creators. Any Ancaster price that competes on tracking loses. Ancaster should *integrate with* MoR affiliate features (read the attribution, or run codes alongside them) rather than replace them.

## 3. Affiliate Recruitment and Activation for Small SaaS

**What works (practitioner evidence, mostly anecdotal):**
- Hand-recruiting people who already create in the category, not waiting for sign-ups. Creem's own guide: "Hand-recruit them. Search '[your category] course' on Maven, Whop, and YouTube."\[21\]
- Recurring commissions. Rewardful's program averages sit at 19–25%.\[26\] Creem, a vendor, claims "30% recurring, lifetime, no cap is the indie SaaS standard in 2026."\[21\]
- Giving top partners training and special campaigns. A Rask AI partnerships lead quoted by Rewardful: "focusing on top performers and helping them grow yields the best results."\[26\]
- Joint webinars and relationship deals over open programs (Indie Hackers).\[23\]
- Patience: 6–12 months of ramp-up.\[26\]

**Marketplaces that match small SaaS with affiliates:**
- Lemon Squeezy Affiliate Hub (bundled, generic)
- Whop Affiliate Marketplace (creator-economy, performance data shown)
- Gumroad's 10% marketplace program
- Dub Partner Network (Enterprise-only)
- PartnerStack marketplace (contract)
- Uppercut (B2B, 13.9% fee on free tier)\[53\]
- Refgrow Referral Exchange,\[35\] LinkJolt Discovery Marketplace, Sled directory (small, new)

AppSumo and openaffiliate.dev-style directories were not verified in this pass; treat them as unassessed.

**Typical performance:** see Section 1. The honest summary: a small program commonly runs on a few dozen active affiliates at most, and the revenue share ranges from negligible to about 20% in vendor case studies. The high end comes from founders who hand-recruited.

**Will founders pay for "we bring you creators who will actually promote you"? Precedents say yes, if it's on results:**
- **Take-rate uplift:** Uppercut charges 13.9% vs 3.9% (network included). PartnerStack charged 3–15%.\[34\]\[53\]\[54\] Dub's network sits in its highest tier.
- **Marketplace commission:** Passionfroot's Help Center says "Brand deals you get from the Partner Network will have a 15% commission" (Stripe fees included), versus 5% on self-sourced storefront deals, which is charged to the brand partner rather than the creator. TechCrunch repeated the 15%/5% split in July 2026 when it reported Passionfroot's $15M Series A led by Insight Partners. SparkLoop takes 20% + 3.5% of publisher earnings from its network.\[55\] Paved takes 30% from publishers.\[18\]
- **Managed-program agencies and retainers:** not priced in this pass; no verified figures.

The consistent pattern is that sourced demand costs 3–4x the take rate of self-sourced demand (5% → 15% at Passionfroot; 3.9% → 13.9% at Uppercut). That differential is Ancaster's pricing model.

**What this means:** An indie founder's realistic paid options are Reddit, ChatGPT Ads (new, low CTR), and one-off newsletter placements. The curated developer networks start at $1,000–$10,000, which prices out most solo founders. MicroConf's data says those founders often can't tell whether ads work at all.\[2\]

**Where a hybrid fits.** Precedents are adjacent but none is complete:
- Dub "program bounties" and "social metrics bounties" (a flat reward for content, alongside commission)\[4\]
- SparkLoop CPA\[16\]
- beehiiv CPC offers\[69\]
- MediaPact's "flat-fee media… with affiliate network integration" (the founder's own description)\[63\]

The gap is **a booked creator placement (flat fee, small) plus a commission on attributed customers, both on the same Referral Receipt.** That fixes both problems MicroConf flags: founders get proof, and creators get guaranteed pay for the work. It is a genuine white space, but no product has validated demand for it yet. Treat it as a hypothesis to test.

## 5. Packaging and Pricing One Account for Both Sides

**Precedents:**
- **Dub** bundles Links into every Partners plan and gates the network to its top tier. It charges subscription plus payout percentage.\[4\]
- **Lemon Squeezy, Gumroad and Whop** charge nothing extra up front and take a percentage of affiliate orders (3%, proportional, or bundled into 3%). One account covers selling, affiliating and promoting.
- **SparkLoop and beehiiv Boosts** make both sides the same account: a newsletter pays to grow and earns by recommending, with a 20% platform take.\[66\]\[70\]
- **Passionfroot** has a storefront for creators and a buyer side for brands, with a 5%/15% split by who sourced the deal.\[19\]\[71\]
- Shopify Collabs, Stripe's multi-product packaging and beehiiv's dashboard were not researched in this pass.

**Price-sensitivity evidence:**
- Entry prices cluster at $19–$49/month.
- New entrants explicitly market "free until your first affiliate" and not paying "$49+ monthly minimum before your program earns anything."\[41\]\[42\]
- Revenue caps are the top complaint against Rewardful and FirstPromoter.\[72\]\[73\]\[74\]
- Tolt's price rises are used against it by competitors.\[75\]
- Dub's 5% payout fee is the main attack line from Rekomi and Uppercut.\[76\]

Conclusion: indie founders will pay a percentage of *results* much more readily than a fixed fee before results. They resent percentages on revenue they generated themselves.

**Recommended packaging:**

| Capability | Free | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| Publisher tools (offer pages, Ledger, permissions, earnings aggregation) | Yes, core | Optional Pro for multi-client/agency later |
| Run an in-house affiliate program (click ID, Stripe promo codes, customer-level attribution, receipts, statements, postbacks, fraud controls) | Yes, unlimited self-invited partners, merchant pays partners directly | — |
| Ancaster Network (listing to curated dev/AI creators, Ancaster-sourced partners) | Listing free | **15% of commissions earned by Ancaster-sourced partners** (merchant pays, on top of the commission) |
| Booked creator placements (flat fee + commission) | — | 10% booking fee on the flat portion |
| Ad serving (script tag / server API, sponsor slots, rotation) | 1 slot free | $19/month flat; server API tier $49/month |

**Traps to avoid:**
1. Charging for tracking or payouts. This competes with free MoR features and $19 tools.
2. Revenue caps on a program the founder built themselves.
3. Custody. Rewardful, Tolt, Dub and Lemon Squeezy all take custody, which brings money-transmission and tax-form burdens. Keep "merchant pays partners" until volume justifies a payout partner.
4. Replacing MoR affiliate features instead of layering on them.
5. Percentage fees on self-sourced partners. That is the exact line competitors attack Dub on.

## 6. The Cascade / Network Cold Start

**Precedents:**
- **Lemon Squeezy and Gumroad:** merchant supply came first from the payments business. The affiliate hub was layered on top, and Lemon Squeezy pitched it as "distribution" because "you know how hard it is to recruit new affiliates."\[43\]
- **Whop:** affiliates on by default on every product, so supply existed instantly. The marketplace then made performance data browsable.\[48\]
- **Dub:** a link tool first. Partners were added later, with the network held back for Enterprise, which suggests the network is still being filled.
- **SparkLoop:** grew through a ConvertKit partnership (acquired 2023), piggybacking an existing base.\[70\]
- **beehiiv Boosts:** native inside a platform where both sides already had accounts.
- **PartnerStack:** built its marketplace from the partner side of its enterprise customers.

The common thread: every network started inside an existing single-player tool with one side already present. None started as an empty marketplace.

**Supply or demand first?** For Ancaster, creators are the scarce side. Merchants have the recruiting and activation pain (PMA/Modash findings; Indie Hackers: "have affiliates ready to go").\[22\] Offers are abundant: thousands of Lemon Squeezy, Whop and Gumroad programs exist.\[77\] So start with creators through the free publisher tools (earnings aggregation plus offer pages already give them a single-player reason to join). Then put Chris's own offers in front of them as the first high-quality, well-supported programs. Advertisers come third, attracted by a visible roster of active dev/AI creators and their receipts.

**How Chris's products seed it:** run each product's program on Ancaster with generous, clear terms (e.g., 30% recurring for 12 months). Give each creator a ready offer page and a code, and publish the aggregate Proof (anonymized clicks → signups → paid) as a public case study. Chris becomes customer zero on both sides.

## 7. Dashboard Information Architecture Check

**Comparison (from features visible this pass):**
- **Dub:** separates the merchant's Partner Program (partners, groups, rewards, bounties, messaging, email campaigns, payouts) from a unified partner dashboard.
- **Lemon Squeezy's affiliate side:** Programs, Clicks, Referrals, Payouts, Creatives.\[78\]
- **Creem's affiliate view:** referral link, total revenue, unique customers, clicks, conversion rate, balance (available/on hold/pending).\[79\]
- **Whop:** puts EPC and conversion rate on marketplace listings.\[47\]
- PartnerStack, Rewardful, beehiiv and Stripe dashboard layouts were not examined in detail this pass.

**Verdict:** Offers / Money / People / Proof is sound and matches how every tool splits the work: programs/links, payouts/balance, partners/affiliates, clicks/referrals. Three adjustments:
1. **Add a fifth, capability-specific "Discover" (or "Network") section.** Creators browse offers; advertisers browse creators and booking slots. Every marketplace product (Whop, Lemon Squeezy Programs, Dub Network) gives discovery its own top-level surface, and it is where Ancaster's paid value lives.
2. **Split Money into "Earned/Owed" and "Pending/Held"** with statuses. Every tool shows hold periods (Lemon Squeezy and Whop use 30 days; Creem shows On Hold), and disputes come from there.
3. **Put EPC and conversion rate at the top of Offers for both sides,** as Whop does. It is the one number both a creator choosing an offer and a founder judging a creator care about.

Keep the same four nouns across capabilities, but change the default sort and empty states by intent.

## 8. Synthesis

**Positioning (one sentence):** "Ancaster runs your affiliate program for free and brings you developer creators who actually ship a page, a code and a receipt for your product. You only pay when they do."

**Decision 1: What's free vs paid**
- A. Freemium SaaS (free up to N partners, then $29–$49/month): competes with $19 tools and free MoR features.
- **B. (Recommended) Free program software; paid only on Ancaster-sourced results and bookings.**
- C. Everything free; monetize only ad serving and creator-side Pro.
- *Decide for me:* B.

**Decision 2: Network fee level**
- A. 10% of sourced-partner commissions.
- **B. (Recommended) 15% of sourced-partner commissions, merchant-paid.** This matches Passionfroot's network rate and sits under SparkLoop's 20%.\[19\]\[55\]
- C. 20% flat.
- *Decide for me:* B, with a 10% founding-advertiser rate for the first 20.

**Decision 3: Hybrid placements**
- A. Commission-only.
- **B. (Recommended) Optional small flat fee ($50–$500 creator-set) plus commission, both on one receipt, 10% booking fee on the flat part.**
- C. Flat fee only, like a Paved clone.
- *Decide for me:* B, but launch it only after 10 creators have made at least one commission-only sale.

**Decision 4: Ad serving**
- A. Bundle free.
- **B. (Recommended) One free slot; $19/month flat; $49/month server API.** The EthicalAds sponsorship platform's $10/month floor shows indie tolerance at this level.\[68\]
- C. Percentage of sponsor revenue.
- *Decide for me:* B.

**Decision 5: MoR integration stance**
- A. Replace MoR affiliates.
- **B. (Recommended) Layer: ingest Stripe/Polar/Creem/Lemon Squeezy/Paddle webhooks and run codes alongside any native program.**
- C. Stripe-only.
- *Decide for me:* B, Stripe and Polar first (Polar has no native affiliates and invites partners).

**Where to find the first 20 indie SaaS advertisers:**
1. Indie Hackers posters asking how to recruit affiliates (search "affiliate program" threads from 2025–2026).
2. Polar sellers, since Polar has no native affiliates.\[80\]
3. Creem and Lemon Squeezy sellers whose native program has no active affiliates.
4. Dev-tool founders who bought EthicalAds/Carbon placements (they have budget and a developer audience).
5. Dub Business-plan startups locked out of the network.
6. Show HN dev/AI tools with a free tier (these fit the "Start free" CTA).
7. Chris's own network on X.

**30-day go-to-market plan**
- **Days 1–7:**
  - Launch Chris's first product's program on Ancaster: Stripe promo codes, offer page, Referral Receipt, 30% recurring for 12 months.
  - Hand-recruit 15 developer/AI creators already covering the category, offering the free publisher tools plus the offer.
- **Days 8–14:**
  - Help each creator ship an Interstitial Standard page and code.
  - Publish a public Proof page (anonymized funnel).
  - Open a "founding advertiser" waitlist: free program, 10% network rate.
- **Days 15–21:**
  - Personally onboard 5 outside indie founders (Polar/Creem sellers first) onto the free program with their existing partners.
  - Offer each 2–3 introductions to creators from the roster.
- **Days 22–30:**
  - Test one hybrid booking (small flat fee plus commission) between a creator and an outside founder.
  - Measure time-to-first-content and time-to-first-paid.
  - Ask every founder: "Would you pay 15% of commissions on creators we bring?" Record the yes/no answers and any objections.

**Kill criteria (at day 30):**
- Fewer than 3 of the 15 recruited creators publish content for Chris's offer. *Kill the network thesis; keep publisher tools.*
- Zero paid conversions attributed through Ancaster receipts on Chris's offers. *Fix product/offer before recruiting advertisers.*
- Fewer than 5 outside founders onboard to the *free* program. *The advertiser side has no pull; pause it.*
- None of the onboarded founders agree to the sourced-partner fee in principle. *Recruitment isn't worth money to this segment; reposition as creator-side only.*
- Any founder demands custody or tax-form handling as a precondition. *Log as a signal for a payout partner, not a reason to build custody.*

## Caveats

- MicroConf's full 2024 report was not accessed; its figures come from MicroConf's own pages citing page numbers. No 2025/2026 edition was found, and the "~700 founders" figure is likely the same survey counted differently. The 29% vs 57% ad-ROI figures conflict.
- Nearly all affiliate-performance and tool-comparison data comes from vendors (Rewardful, Dub, Rekomi, Refgrow, Cello, Affonso, Uppercut, LinkJolt, Tapfiliate), and most of those sell competing products. Some prices conflict across sources (PromoteKit, LinkMink, Endorsely).\[31\]\[32\]\[33\]\[36\] Confirm on the vendor page before quoting.
- PartnerStack pricing is estimated by third parties and its marketplace size claims are inconsistent.\[81\]\[82\]\[83\]
- ChatGPT Ads pricing is moving monthly; CPC/CPM/CTR figures come from agencies and blogs, not OpenAI.\[13\]\[14\]\[15\]
- Not verified this pass: podcast, X, Product Hunt, GitHub Sponsors/OpenCollective, Swapstack, AppSumo, openaffiliate.dev, Shopify Collabs, beehiiv/Stripe/PartnerStack/Rewardful dashboard layouts, and agency retainer pricing.
- The 15% network fee, hybrid booking, and $19/$49 ad-serving prices are recommendations inferred from precedents, not validated demand.
