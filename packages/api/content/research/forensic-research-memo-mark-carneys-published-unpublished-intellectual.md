# Forensic Research Memo: Mark Carney's Published and Unpublished Intellectual Work, and the Broader Orbit

## Preliminary Note on Framing

A clarification is necessary at the outset. The query's working hypothesis — that the "pulled second book" was a Reith Lectures volume from William Collins announced in late 2020 / early 2021 — does not match the documentary record. The William Collins volume based on (and contemporaneous with) the 2020 Reith Lectures was **published**, in 2021, as *Value(s): Building a Better World for All* (in Canada *Value(s): Building a Better World for All*; in the UK initially as *Value(s): Building a Better World for All* and reissued in paperback in October 2021 as *Value(s): Climate, Credit, Covid and How We Focus on What Matters*, ISBN 9780008522025). The four Reith Lectures correspond directly to four of the book's chapters / parts.

The book that has actually been pulled / repeatedly delayed is Carney's **second** book, **The Hinge: Time to Build an Even Better Canada** (Signal / McClelland & Stewart, an imprint of Penguin Random House Canada), announced in October 2024 for May 13, 2025 release, then pushed to July 1, 2025, then disappeared from publisher pages, with Amazon at one point showing a January 2026 placeholder. This memo therefore re-organises Thread 1 around *The Hinge* (the genuinely shelved volume) while still providing the full Reith Lectures content the query requested, since the Reith Lectures *are* the policy DNA later absorbed into *Value(s)* and contradicted by Carney's actions as PM.

## THREAD 1: THE PULLED / DELAYED SECOND BOOK — *THE HINGE*

### 1.1 Publisher announcement and listings

- **Announced:** October 3, 2024, by Penguin Random House Canada / Signal / McClelland & Stewart. Reported by Canadian Press and carried by The Globe and Mail, National Newswatch, Castanet, Lakeland Today and others under the headline "Mark Carney to release book 'The Hinge', a look at Canada's 'path forward.'"
- **Title:** *The Hinge: Time to Build an Even Better Canada*
- **Imprint:** Signal (Penguin Random House Canada / McClelland & Stewart)
- **Hardcover ISBN (PRH Canada listing):** 9780771024955
- **Original publication date:** May 13, 2025 (with some retailer pages listing May 15)
- **Carney's own X/Twitter announcement** (October 3, 2024): "Excited that my new book, The Hinge, will be published by Signal in May 2025. It's all about how we can to build an even better Canada in an increasingly dangerous and divided world. Pre-order it here: @McClellandBooks" (link to penguinrandomhouse.ca/books/787646/the-hinge-by-penguin-random-house/9780771024955).
- **Marketing blurb (per Quebec retailer Archambault and PRH preview text):** the book "charts an ambitious and urgent path forward for Canada and the world as we collectively face a multitude of existential threats to our long-standing democratic traditions… We are at a hinge moment in history — reminiscent of what the Allies faced at the end of 1941, when Winston Churchill was in Ottawa meeting with Mackenzie King — an age of uncertainty and rising perils that is reshaping global political, economic, technological, and social orders." The blurb invokes "Churchill, King and FDR" eventually swinging "the Hinge of Fate" and frames Canadian action as something Canadians do "not as a government, but as a people."

### 1.2 The delay / shelving

- **April 2025 — first delay confirmed.** Juno News (April 2025) reported under the headline "EXCLUSIVE: Carney's publishers won't say why his new book was delayed": "The publishers of Liberal Leader Mark Carney's forthcoming book are refusing to comment on why the release of the title has been delayed from its original release date of May 15. With the new release date set for July 1, it remains unclear whether the book will be published as advertised. Carney's book held the working title *The Hinge: Time to Build an Even Better Canada* but it was mysteriously shelved with no official explanation from the publisher McClelland & Stewart, with all presale and order links leading to a dead end."
- **Publisher communication on PRH Canada site:** the page replaced book details with the placeholder, "Information about this exciting publication from Penguin Random House Canada is coming soon."
- **Toronto Star explanation (anonymous source, May/June 2025):** the delay was attributed to Carney having not finished "the editing process in the last several months as he campaigned." The Star also reported Carney had finished writing the book.
- **Alternative explanation reported by Ken Whyte (Substack, "Shush," 2025):** "I heard the delay had more to do with campaign finance rules that would consider a book publicized or released in election season as political advertising… Amazon now has *The Hinge* coming next January. Carney's political opponents have been enjoying the delay. Critics both left and right have attributed it to the difficulty of squaring positions taken by Carney a year or two ago with positions he espoused during the campaign."
- **Critical journalism on the disappearance:** Terence Corcoran (Financial Post / Yahoo Finance, "The (missing) book on Mark Carney's plan for Canada," 2025): "Alas, the book will not be published as promised on May 13, apparently because Carney never got around to the final edit of the manuscript. Another possible reason for holding back on publishing *The Hinge* is that it would reveal too much detail about Carney's interventionist master plan for the Canadian economy." Corcoran also reproduces a passage from *Value(s)* relevant to the contradiction problem: "A belief in free trade may be totemic among economists, but while trade makes countries better off, it does not raise all boats within them."
- **Status as of mid-2025 / 2026:** Pre-order links broken; PRH page wiped; Amazon Canada at one point listed January 2026; no confirmed new release. Publishers have declined comment.

### 1.4 The 2020 Reith Lectures — full content (the policy DNA later absorbed into *Value(s)*)

The lectures aired on BBC Radio 4 in late 2020 under the series title **"How We Get What We Value,"** presented by Anita Anand, produced by Jim Frank, edited by Hugh Levinson. They map one-to-one onto *Value(s)*'s structure (Part I on the crisis of value, Part II on three crises, Part III on reclaiming values).

**Lecture 1 — "From Moral to Market Sentiments"** (December 2, 2020). Argument: traces an arc from Adam Smith's *Theory of Moral Sentiments* in 18th-century Glasgow to a present where "the value of something, some act or someone is equated with its monetary value." Carney structures this around three "paradoxes of value": (a) essentials like water are cheap while luxuries like diamonds are expensive; (b) financial markets value Amazon at $1.5 trillion but assign no value to the Amazon rainforest; (c) essential workers (nurses, carers) are systematically underpriced. He uses Oscar Wilde's aphorism ("knows the price of everything, the value of nothing") and the famous Israeli daycare / charity-fundraising experiment (children raising money for charity raised more without financial incentives) to argue that markets crowd out moral motivation. The lecture endorses "objective" theories of value (Aristotle, Smith, Marx) over "subjective" / neoclassical theories (Jevons, Marshall) — a striking gesture from a former Goldman MD and central banker.

**Lecture 2 — "From Credit Crisis to Resilience"** (December 9, 2020). Re-tells the 2008 financial crisis and Carney's role in the post-crisis Basel III / FSB reforms. He attacks "the three lies of finance": (1) "This time is different"; (2) "Markets always clear"; (3) "Markets are moral." Argues the system remains "ill-prepared for the next crisis" without vigilance, that banker culture must internalise values of solidarity, responsibility, humility, and that "too big to fail" was never truly resolved. Calls for stronger systemic-risk regulation, ring-fencing, and capital buffers — a recommendation set markedly tougher than what Carney as PM has so far signalled toward Canadian banks.

**Lecture 3 — "From Covid Crisis to Renaissance"** (December 16, 2020). Argues the pandemic exposed the consequences of states having "undervalued resilience" — pandemic preparedness, public health, supply-chain redundancy. Names "essential workers" as undervalued, calls out the trend over "decades" of "risk being steadily downloaded onto individuals," and explicitly invokes Thomas Hobbes's *Leviathan* on the state's protective duty: "the fundamental duty of the state is to protect" — including "promoting financial stability, protecting the environment, maintaining data privacy, and yes, preparing for a pandemic." Argues we are "moving relentlessly from a market economy to a market society" and that COVID "could help reverse that, so that public values help shape private value." Discusses cross-national differences in the implicit financial value placed on a human life.

**Lecture 4 — "From Climate Crisis to Real Prosperity"** (December 23, 2020). The most prescriptive lecture. Core claims:
- "The roots of the climate change threat lie in a deeper crisis of values."
- Three diagnostic frames: "human frailties" produce a "tragedy of the horizon"; "market failures" produce a "tragedy of the commons"; the "flattening of values" makes everything subordinate to price.
- "There are two solutions to the tragedy of the commons: property rights and pollution pricing" — i.e., **carbon pricing is presented as one of the two foundational solutions to climate change**.
- Calls for mandatory TCFD-aligned climate disclosures by all companies and investors, "giving shareholders power to control companies' carbon footprints," and implies shareholders should be able to veto management plans not aligned with net zero by 2050.
- Endorses social movements — explicitly cites Greta Thunberg and Fridays for Future — as the political mechanism by which net zero can be made "the apex" of "the hierarchy of values."
- Insists "continued growth isn't a fairy tale, it's a necessity," but says GDP "was devised a century ago when the earth seemed immortal" and must be replaced.
- Frames the transition as requiring a state-orchestrated reorientation of finance.

### 1.5 Did *Value(s)* absorb the Reith material?

Yes, comprehensively. The *Value(s)* table of contents (Signal / McClelland & Stewart 2021 edition) shows Part I — "How We Got Here" (theories of value, the market society), Part II — "Three Crises of Value" (the credit crisis, the COVID crisis, the climate crisis, with explicit "Tragedy of the Horizon" chapter), and Part III — "Reclaiming Our Values" (Values-Based Leadership; How Purposeful Companies Create Value; Investing for Value(s); How Canada Can Build Value for All; Conclusion: Humility). Each Reith Lecture maps onto a specific chapter cluster. The book is not a transcription but a substantial expansion (~500+ pages). The 2021 UK paperback reissue retitled *Value(s): Climate, Credit, Covid and How We Focus on What Matters* (ISBN 9780008522025, William Collins, October 28, 2021) makes the Reith correspondence explicit in its very subtitle.

## THREAD 2: VALUE(S) — COMPREHENSIVE FORENSIC AUDIT

### 2.1 Specific claims in the book and their tension with Carney's record as PM

**(a) Carbon pricing.** In Lecture 4 and in the *Value(s)* climate chapters, Carney identifies carbon pricing as one of only two structural solutions to the tragedy of the commons (alongside property rights). The Kirkus review captures the book's stance: Carney "urges community building and infrastructure development, including stricter regulations for carbon taxes, all overseen by the state. 'Nations — not companies — must set these ground rules for markets to be fair,' he writes." **Contradiction in office:** within days of taking the oath of office on March 14, 2025, Carney signed an order cancelling the consumer carbon tax, including the scheduled April 1, 2025 increase (Gowling WLG advisory, March 14, 2025). He retained only the industrial output-based pricing system. He spent the 2025 campaign attacking Pierre Poilievre on climate rhetoric ("Pierre Poilievre would let our planet burn") while removing the carbon price he had championed for a decade as central banker, UN envoy, and author.

**(b) The role of the state vs markets.** Carney in *Value(s)* writes that the state's role has been extended "to areas as varied as promoting financial stability, protecting the environment, maintaining data privacy, and yes, preparing for a pandemic," and that "nations — not companies — must set these ground rules." He explicitly frames the COVID experience as evidence that "we are moving relentlessly from a market economy to a market society" which must be reversed "so that public values help shape private value." **Tension in office:** Carney's first months as PM included reversing Trudeau's planned capital-gains inclusion-rate hike (returning a benefit primarily to higher earners and corporations), expanding the Indigenous Loan Guarantee Program from $5 billion to $10 billion to underwrite private capital (including for sectors Brookfield operates in), endorsing a new West Coast oil pipeline and a $16.5-billion Athabasca carbon-capture system, and signing a November 2025 MOU with the Government of Alberta on new pipeline and CCS infrastructure — a programme more recognisably market-enabling than the "market society reversal" of the book.

**(c) Inequality.** *Value(s)* is described in the Kirkus review as an extended call for "solidarity and the enhancement of the social capital on which economic capital relies for its long-term health." Diana Fox Carney's parliamentary testimony in 2013 (cited in Globe and Mail / Yahoo profiles) makes the same case: "One of the key features of income inequality is that it tends to be self-reinforcing, so that the poorest groups are less able to invest in their children and in their health and education and in other things that make for success." **Tension in office:** Canadian Dimension's review of *Value(s)* anticipates exactly the gap: "When he talks about wealth inequality, he avoids discussion of closing tax loopholes and increasing taxes on the rich — in fact he advocates for lowering taxes on so-called start-ups. When he talks about stagnant incomes, he's silent about raising the minimum wage and increasing the power of unions." Carney's actual record in office has been consistent with the book's silences (capital-gains reversal, no minimum-wage move, no union-strengthening package), not with its rhetoric of solidarity.

**(d) Financial regulation and the "three lies of finance."** *Value(s)* (Chapter on credit crisis) and Reith Lecture 2 argue the post-2008 reforms are incomplete and vigilance is required. **Tension:** Carney's own Brookfield tenure (Vice-Chair from 2020; Chair from 2024 through resignation in January 2025) involved leading the firm's ESG / impact strategy at exactly the moment Carney had to retract Brookfield's claim that its $600 billion portfolio was carbon-neutral (the claim was based on "all the avoided emissions" from renewables) — a textbook instance of the kind of climate-finance "lies" he warned about in the book. The Brookfield Global Transition Fund (BGTF, $15 billion, 2021) and Brookfield Global Transition Fund II (BGTF II, $10 billion, 2024) — both **co-headed by Carney with Connor Teskey** per Brookfield's own press releases — were registered in Bermuda via BGTF Bermuda GP Ltd. and BGTF II Bermuda GP Ltd. (Ontario Business Registry, surfaced by Radio-Canada / CBC March 2025). Asked whether registering an environmental-transition fund in a tax haven was ethical, Carney told reporters in Windsor: "I understand how the world works and how these structures work." The book's prescription that "markets must be re-embedded in values" and that nations must set the rules sits awkwardly beside the structuring choice he personally signed off on.

**(e) Corporate responsibility / asset managers.** Chapter 14 ("How Purposeful Companies Create Value") extols what Carney called "purposeful" firms and praises figures such as Emmanuel Faber (then Danone CEO). The Thinking Faith review notes that Faber was ousted by activist shareholders during the book's production — a fact Carney himself acknowledges in the book — yet the book's prescriptive framework remained that of voluntary "purpose," not binding constraints. The Canadian Dimension review summarises: "Carney has no interest in challenging the power of corporate oligarchy; instead, he wants to use market incentives to encourage oligarchs to act in a pro-social fashion."

**(f) Democratic accountability.** Carney's COVID chapter calls states' protective role "fundamental" and praises the social movements (Reith 4) that hold democratic systems to account. **Tension:** Carney became Liberal Party leader on March 9, 2025 (85.9% on the first ballot, with the vote conducted only among Liberal Party members), was sworn in as PM on March 14 without having held any elected office at any level, and held a snap election shortly afterward — entering the office of Prime Minister entirely on the basis of an intra-party caucus and membership process. The Britannica entry phrases the same fact more dryly: "Carney became his party's leader with no experience in elected office."

**(g) Tax fairness.** *Value(s)* repeatedly invokes "fairness" as one of the seven core values (alongside solidarity, resilience, responsibility, sustainability, humility, and dynamism — per the Jacobin review by Doug Henwood / staff). **Tension:** as Connor Teskey's co-chair on BGTF/BGTF II, the funds chose Bermuda, where corporate income tax is zero. NDP leader Jagmeet Singh (CBC, March 26, 2025): Carney "chose to register [the funds] in another country to avoid paying those taxes. That's less money for health care. Less money for seniors." Silas Xuereb of Canadians for Tax Fairness called it "ironic that environmental funds were registered in a tax haven." A 2023 report by the Centre for International Corporate Tax Accountability and Research (CICTAR) had previously criticised Brookfield's tax structuring including Bermuda usage; a 2017 Toronto Star investigation estimated Brookfield had avoided roughly $5 billion in taxes globally.

### 2.2 Critical / unsparing reviews

- **Canadian Dimension — "I read Mark Carney's book so you don't have to"** (the most cutting critique from the Canadian left): "Of a neoliberalism with 'values.' A capitalism that chooses to be sensible and responsible. A corporate technocracy that resolves the twin crises of declining living standards and environmental catastrophe without slowing capital accumulation… When he talks about the dangers of a market dominated by oligopolies, he refuses to talk about using the power of the state to break up large companies… As the title of *Value(s)* suggests, Carney's response to the excesses of capitalism is moral scolding."
- **Jacobin — "Capitalists Can't Become Nicer"** (international left): "This consummate insider can't trace the roots of those problems back to their fundamental sources. Progressive-minded elites of today believe that companies can generate profit without undermining social or environmental benefits."
- **The New Dark Age / William Bowles** (March 2021): contrasts Carney's Goldman Sachs decade — "where he played a prominent role in advising the black majority government of South Africa on issuing international bonds and he was active for the company during the Russian debt crisis of 1998" — with the book's moralism, asking how that history "built a better world for all."
- **Goodreads composite reader reviews:** repeatedly flag the book as "overly long," "very dry," and "jarringly self-aggrandizing (despite preaching the importance of humility on a number of occasions)." Note that "by far the most negative and critical chapter in the book is about cryptocurrencies" — i.e., the one sector outside the central-banker comfort zone is the one Carney savages, while Greenspan and Wall Street are largely forgiven.
- **Spark Solidarity** (March 2025): argues *Value(s)* "functions more as public relations than as a blueprint for systemic reform" — directly echoing Jacobin.
- **National Review** (April 2021, behind paywall but listed): conservative critique focused on the problem that "there is no assurance the best [values] will rise to the top, especially in a crisis."
- **Kirkus** (early 2021, broadly positive but identifies the move): "an argument that will send Chicago School acolytes into despair." Useful as confirmation of the book's stated stance, against which the contradictions can be measured.

Internal contradictions identified by reviewers (not just contradictions with the record):
- Praises Faber and "purposeful companies" while acknowledging Faber was ousted by shareholders.
- Preaches humility throughout while writing in what readers describe as a self-aggrandising register.
- Endorses tougher financial regulation while having spent the writing period at Brookfield, a firm whose tax practices CICTAR was simultaneously documenting.
- Calls for the state to "set the rules" while spending most of the book on voluntary frameworks (GFANZ, TCFD-style disclosure, "purposeful capitalism").

### 2.3 Intellectual sources

Acknowledged debts in the text and reviews:
- **Adam Smith** — *Theory of Moral Sentiments* (frame for Lecture 1).
- **Michael Sandel** — *What Money Can't Buy* (the market-economy-to-market-society move is Sandel's, openly).
- **Mariana Mazzucato** — *The Value of Everything* (the subjective/objective value distinction and critique of value extraction is Mazzucato's; Carney's structure largely tracks hers).
- **Aristotle, Aquinas, Marx, Jevons, Marshall** — name-checked for objective vs subjective value.
- **Sir William Petty, Bernardo Davanzati** — used in the historical chapter on the paradox of value.
- **Hobbes** — *Leviathan* invoked in the COVID lecture/chapter on state protection.
- **Jean Tirole** — *Economics for the Common Good* (Thinking Faith review identifies him as the closest parallel volume).
- **Oscar Wilde** — the price/value aphorism that runs through the whole project.

Conspicuously thin or absent acknowledgements identified by critics:
- **Karl Polanyi** (*The Great Transformation*) — Carney's central claim that markets must be "re-embedded" in social values is the Polanyi argument, but Polanyi receives nothing like the discussion the move warrants.
- **Hyman Minsky** — given the entire credit-crisis chapter is about endogenous financial fragility, Minsky's near-absence is notable.
- **Wolfgang Streeck, Colin Crouch** — on the relationship between democracy and markets (the explicit subject of Carney's argument), they go uncited.
- **Thomas Piketty** — the inequality material is hard to write without Piketty; *Value(s)* gives him only glancing treatment.
- **The "Foundational Economy" school** (Manchester, Cardiff) — directly relevant to Carney's "essential workers" argument, not engaged.
- **The Jacobin and Canadian Dimension reviews** both make the point that Carney's framework is a sanitised re-presentation of Sandel and Mazzucato without their political teeth.

### 2.4 Promotional-era Carney quotes on his own contribution

From the William Collins blurb / endorsements pack and interview circuit (2021):
- Promoted by his publisher with endorsements positioning him as "one of the great global thinkers of our time."
- The Amazon UK Kindle promotional copy reproduces a Carney TV-interview quote into the marketing text: "The message from the financial markets is there's a limit to unfunded spending and unfunded tax cuts in this environment" — used to position the book as policy commentary on the 2022 Truss "mini-budget" crisis, even though the book pre-dates it.
- Sub-titling discipline tells the story: the Canadian/US edition is *Building a Better World for All*; the William Collins UK paperback is *An Economist's Guide to Everything That Matters* (a deliberately grandiose framing); the 2021 second UK paperback retitles to *Climate, Credit, Covid and How We Focus on What Matters* — three different positionings of the same content for three audiences.
- Carney's later self-positioning is captured in his Liberal-leadership-era self-description (cited in CBC interview, February 2025) explaining why he had earlier declined Stephen Harper's 2012 offer to become finance minister: it "wasn't appropriate" to "go directly from being governor into elective politics" — a standard he relaxed for himself in 2025.

## THREAD 3: BROADER ORBIT — WHAT'S BEEN MISSED

### 3.1 The Century Initiative

- **What it is:** A registered charity / lobbying group founded by **Mark Wiseman** and **Dominic Barton** (originally as the Laurier Project Foundation in 2009, rebranded ~2011–2014) advocating that Canada's population grow to **100 million by 2100**, primarily via immigration; concentrating growth in "megaregions" (GTA, Greater Vancouver, NCR).
- **Funding / supporters** documented by Dominion Review's reporting and Wikipedia: support from former PC PM Brian Mulroney, advisors to former Finance Minister Bill Morneau, and historically a heavy McKinsey footprint (in 2016, four of five volunteers were McKinsey-employed; the inaugural executive director Aleema Jamal was ex-McKinsey).
- **Carney connection (documented):** Carney appointed **Mark Wiseman — chair of the Century Initiative board** — to the Council of Advisors on Canada-U.S. Relations on March 20, 2025, within days of being sworn in. Order Paper Question Q-425 (Conservative MP Marilyn Gladu) showed Wiseman met with the PM personally within days of taking office, again in April 2025, and again in September 2025 (per Rebel News reporting on the OPQ response). In February 2026, Carney appointed Wiseman as **Canadian Ambassador to the United States**, replacing Kirsten Hillman, with effect from mid-February 2026.
- **Carney's own public position on Century Initiative** (per National Observer fact-check, April 4, 2025): Carney distanced himself from the 100-million target during the 2025 campaign, said he would keep the existing immigration caps (395,000 permanent residents for 2025, falling to 365,000 by 2027) and would not increase levels until "we've expanded housing, we've reabsorbed [people]." He has not, however, severed the Wiseman connection.

### 3.2 Mark Wiseman — biography and role

Drawn from Mark Wiseman Wikipedia, Yahoo Finance / Financial Post profile, Grokipedia, and Deep Dive reporting (all 2025–2026):
- Born 1970, Niagara Falls, Ontario. Queen's BA, U of T joint JD/MBA, Yale LLM as a Fulbright Scholar; clerked for Justice Beverley McLachlin; began career at Sullivan & Cromwell (New York / Paris).
- Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan (2000s); Canada Pension Plan Investment Board (joined 2005, **CEO 2012–2016**); **Senior Managing Director, BlackRock, Global Head of Active Equities, Chairman of Alternatives, member of Global Executive Committee (2016–2019)** — at one point briefly speculated as a possible Larry Fink successor.
- **Departed BlackRock December 5, 2019** after failing to disclose a consensual relationship with a subordinate, in violation of BlackRock's "relationships at work" policy (Wall Street Journal, Al Jazeera, December 2019).
- **Co-founder, FCLTGlobal** ("Focusing Capital on the Long Term"), set up by BlackRock, CPPIB, Dow, McKinsey & Company and Tata in 2016.
- **Chair of Alberta Investment Management Corporation (AIMCo) 2020–2023**; ousted along with the rest of the board by the Alberta government in 2024 citing underperformance.
- **Co-founder and Board Chair, Century Initiative.**
- 2016 member of **Bill Morneau's Advisory Council on Economic Growth** (chaired by Barton).
- **Senior Advisor / Chairman, Lazard Canada; Chairman, Alter Domus; Senior Advisor, Boston Consulting Group; Board member, NOVA Chemicals; Senior Advisor, Hillhouse Capital** (Chinese-affiliated private-equity firm).
- April 2024 Globe and Mail op-ed: "Simply put, we are falling further behind in our standard of living without realizing it. Canada has become a nation of complacency."
- Described in Canadian press as a "close friend of Prime Minister Mark Carney."

The Wiseman appointments concentrate a single individual at the intersection of: BlackRock alumnus → CPPIB ex-CEO → Hillhouse / China link → Century Initiative immigration lobby → Lazard / BCG → Carney's principal Canada-U.S. trade and economic envoy.

### 3.3 BlackRock

- **No documented Carney board or formal advisory seat at BlackRock.** What is documented:
  - Carney's repeated public stages with Larry Fink at WEF / Davos.
  - The BlackRock–Brookfield commercial relationship runs through Wiseman.
  - GFANZ, co-launched by Carney at COP26 (November 2021), included BlackRock as a foundational asset-manager participant.
- **BlackRock's role in Canadian infrastructure:** in 2016 the federal Liberal government's investment-attraction work (during the Morneau / Barton ACEG era) produced a partnership with BlackRock that has been continuously criticised. Carney was not a federal official at that point but was a sustained adviser to Trudeau and the Liberals.

### 3.4 Trilateral Commission

- The Trilateral connection is asserted in opinion / advocacy commentary (Patrick Wood / Technocracy News; The Hill opinion piece by columnist) and treated as common knowledge by Carney critics, but I have **not located a primary-source listing of Carney as a member of the Trilateral Commission**. The Trilateral does not publish a comprehensive membership list publicly. **Status: not independently documented in this research.** WEF Foundation Board membership, G30 membership, Group of Seven Council, Chatham House chair, Bilderberg attendance, and Hoffman Institute / INSEAD board memberships are documented; Trilateral is not.

### 3.5 Pierre Elliott Trudeau Foundation

- The Trudeau Foundation has been embroiled in a 2022–2023 scandal over a $200,000 donation from Chinese political strategist Zhang Bin connected to alleged CCP interference; most of its board resigned in early 2023.
- **No documentary evidence in this research of Mark Carney being a Member, Fellow, Mentor, board member, or scholarship recipient of the Trudeau Foundation.** His name does not appear in the Foundation's published lists. (Past mentors include Beverly McLachlin, Louise Arbour, Ed Broadbent, Elizabeth May, Frank Iacobucci, Anne McLellan, etc.) **Status: no documented connection.**
