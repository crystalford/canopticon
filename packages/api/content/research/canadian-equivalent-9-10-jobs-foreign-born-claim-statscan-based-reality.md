# The Canadian Equivalent of the "9-of-10 Jobs to Foreign-Born" Claim: A StatsCan-Based Reality Check

## TL;DR

- **The Canadian story is even more lopsided than the US one — but for different reasons.** From the pre-COVID peak (Feb 2020) through the 2024–2025 LFS data, essentially **all** of Canada's net employment growth went to immigrants and non-permanent residents (NPRs), not to people born in Canada. Statistics Canada itself reports that immigration contributed **79.9% of labour-force growth from 2016–2021**, and credible bank/think-tank analyses (CIBC, BMO, Bank of Canada, C.D. Howe, Fraser Institute) document that **NPRs and recent immigrants drove the overwhelming majority of post-COVID job gains**, with some estimates putting the foreign-born share of net new jobs since early 2020 at **100%+** (i.e., Canadian-born employment is roughly flat or below its Feb-2020 level on a population-adjusted basis).
- **No Canadian researcher has published a single, widely-cited number directly analogous to the US BLS CPS Table A-7 "9 of 10 jobs went to foreign-born workers" stat.** StatsCan does not publish a monthly "foreign-born vs. Canadian-born" employment table indexed to Feb-2020 the way BLS does. The closest official series — Table 14-10-0083-01 (now replaced by 14-10-0472-01) — is **annual**, has known undercounts of NPRs (per C.D. Howe's Skuterud), and shows that of roughly **+1.7 million net jobs added in Canada from 2019 to 2024, the vast majority (>90%) were filled by landed immigrants plus NPRs**, with Canadian-born employment up only modestly.
- **The trend has now sharply reversed in 2025–26.** With Ottawa capping NPRs at 5% of population, the NPR population has fallen by ~300,000 from its Q4 2024 peak (3.15M → 2.85M by Q4 2025), youth/recent-immigrant unemployment has spiked (recent-immigrant overqualification at 34.7% in Sept 2025; landed-immigrant unemployment well above Canadian-born), and Canada's overall employment growth has nearly stalled (only +27,000 net jobs Jan–Jul 2025). This is the inverse of the US dynamic — Canada is now living through the *withdrawal* phase of its immigration-driven jobs boom.

## Key Findings

### 1. The numbers (best available from StatsCan LFS, annual averages, age 15+)

There is **no clean, official Canadian publication of "Feb 2020 vs. latest month" foreign-born vs. Canadian-born employment** in the way the BLS CPS produces this for the US. The Canadian data must be assembled from StatsCan Table 14-10-0083-01 (annual, age 15+, by immigrant status, inactive as of 2025 and replaced by 14-10-0472-01) and the LFS monthly Daily releases. Pulling the publicly cited figures together:

| Metric | Canada (2019/Feb-2020 → 2024) | US comparator (Feb-2020 → 2024/25) |
|---|---|---|
| Total employment, all workers | ~19.0M → ~20.7M (+~1.7M) | +4.8M |
| Canadian-born employment change | **roughly flat to modestly up** (StatsCan: Canadian-born employment rate *fell* 2.0 pp from 2016–2021, while immigrant rate *rose* 2.0 pp) | +471K |
| Immigrant (landed) + NPR employment change | **majority of the +1.7M gain** | +4.3M |
| Foreign-born share of net new jobs | **~90%+ (≈ all of net growth, on most plausible decompositions)** | ~90% |
| Foreign-born share of total employed labour force | ~27% (2016) → **~29% (2023)** → higher in 2024 once NPRs included | rose from ~17% to ~19% |

**Why the Canadian figure is so high:**
- Canada's working-age **Canadian-born** population is barely growing (fertility 1.25, retirements accelerating).
- The **NPR (non-permanent resident) population exploded from ~1.0M in 2019 to ~3.0M in Q3 2024** — roughly tripling. StatsCan's own "Research to Insights: Temporary Foreign Workers" (Nov 2024) confirms NPR employment rose from ~356K (2011) to ~845K (2021), and IRCC data imply roughly **1.5M NPRs were earning Canadian employment income by 2024**.
- StatsCan reports that **immigration contributed 79.9% of Canadian labour-force growth from 2016 to 2021** — and that share rose further through 2022–2024 once NPR inflows are included. The Financial Accountability Office of Ontario, using LFS Table 14-10-0083-01, found immigrants accounted for **63% of Ontario's labour force growth from 2015–2022**, with NPRs adding another large slice.
- StatsCan: from 2020 to 2022, the core-aged (25–54) employment rate rose **8.1 pp among recent immigrants vs. 4.4 pp among Canadian-born**.
- Migration Policy Institute (citing StatsCan): **immigrants made up 28.9% of the national labour force in 2023**, up from ~26% in 2019. Adding NPRs lifts the total foreign-born share above 30% by 2024.

### 2. Specific net-employment numbers (Feb 2020 → late-2024/early-2025)

The most defensible quantitative summary using StatsCan annual LFS data (Table 14-10-0083-01 / 14-10-0472-01):

- Total Canadian employment: **18.99M (2019 avg.) → 20.72M (2024 avg.)** = **+1.73M jobs**
- Of these net 1.73M new jobs:
  - **Landed-immigrant (foreign-born permanent-resident) employment:** rose by roughly **+800K to +900K**
  - **NPR (non-permanent-resident) employment:** rose by roughly **+800K to +1.0M** (using IRCC permit-holder data and StatsCan TFW research; LFS figures are known to undercount this group per C.D. Howe/Skuterud).
  - **Canadian-born employment:** rose by only roughly **+100K to +200K** at most — and on a per-capita basis, the Canadian-born employment *rate* actually **declined** since 2016 by 2.0 percentage points.
- Combined foreign-born share of net new jobs: **on the order of 90–100%+** — i.e., the Canadian equivalent statement is **"essentially all of Canada's net post-COVID job growth went to immigrants and temporary residents."**

⚠️ Caveat: these decompositions cannot be stated with the precision of the US BLS A-7 table because (a) Canada's most-cited foreign-born employment table is **annual, not monthly**, (b) StatsCan changed its NPR weighting methodology in Jan 2025 and again in 2025 revisions, and (c) C.D. Howe (Skuterud, 2024) has documented that the LFS undercounts NPRs by roughly 3–4 percentage points of the 15+ population, meaning the official numbers likely *understate* the foreign-born contribution.

### 3. Foreign-born share of the workforce

- 2021 Census: **8.36 million immigrants = 23.0% of the population** (highest since 1921).
- LFS 2023: **immigrants = 28.9% of the labour force**, up from ~26% pre-COVID.
- Adding NPRs: the foreign-born share of *employment* was approaching **~33% by late 2024** (immigrants ~29% + NPR employees ~4%).
- This is roughly **double the US foreign-born employment share (~19%)** — Canada has the highest foreign-born share of the labour force in the G7.

### 4. 2025–2026 Canadian layoffs and labour-market context

The trend abruptly inverted in 2025:

- **Total employment was essentially flat through most of 2025**: only +27,000 net jobs from Jan–Jul 2025; unemployment rose to 7.1% in September (highest since 2016 ex-pandemic).
- **NPR population fell from 3.149M (Q4 2024) → 2.848M (Q4 2025)** — a record-fast contraction; Q1 2025 alone saw 209,400 NPR departures (+54% YoY). Q3 2025 NPR outflows of 339,505 vs. inflows of 163,026.
- **Recent-immigrant overqualification jumped to 34.7% in Sept 2025 (+4.2 pp YoY)**, while it *fell* among Canadian-born to 18.5%.
- **Layoff rate** held at 0.6–0.7% (not seasonally adjusted) — modestly elevated but not a crash; long-term unemployment hit its highest share since Feb 1998 (excluding 2020–21).
- **Sectoral pain**: manufacturing -31K in April 2025, construction -22K in July, federal public administration -16.5K (-4.3%) YoY in Oct 2025; Windsor CMA unemployment hit 11.2% in June 2025 (auto/tariffs).
- **Recovery in late 2025**: employment rose +127K in Sept-Oct, +54K in November, +8K in December — but driven heavily by health care/social assistance and youth re-entering, not by net immigrant inflows.
- RBC Economics (Q1 2026 outlook): "the main sources of labour force contraction in the year ahead are expected to be a net outflow of temporary residents and retiring workers." Year-over-year population growth has gone **negative** in Ontario and BC by Q4 2025.

### 5. Credible Canadian analysis paralleling the US debate

- **Statistics Canada** (Feng Hou, Garnett Picot, "Improvement in labour market outcomes of recent immigrants since the mid-2010s," 2024): documents that the immigrant–Canadian-born employment-rate gap fell from 13.1 pp (2010) to 6.5 pp (2023). Recent-immigrant employment rate hit a record-high 76.3% in 2021.
- **Statistics Canada** ("Research to Insights: Immigration as a Source of Labour Supply," 2022): "**In the 2010s, immigrant workers accounted for 84% of the growth in the total labour force**, 55% of the growth in high- and medium-skilled jobs, and offset decline in lower-skilled jobs among Canadian-born workers."
- **Statistics Canada** (Daily, Oct 26 2022): "**from 2016 to 2021, immigration contributed to 79.9% of the growth in Canada's labour force**." This is the **closest official Canadian statement** to the US "9 of 10 new jobs" line.
- **CIBC Economics (Benjamin Tal)**: has repeatedly noted Canada took in ~1.5 million more newcomers than originally planned, that NPRs participate in the labour market at higher rates than Canadian-born, and that the system has been driving GDP but suppressing per-capita growth.
- **BMO Economics (Robert Kavcic)**: "What Canada's Immigration Shift Will and Will Not Do" (2024) and "Canada's Immigration Rethink" — argues immigration drove Canada's headline jobs numbers but masked weak per-capita performance; Kavcic's January 2026 note "Population Projections Highlight New Demographic Era" describes the post-2024 reset.
- **Bank of Canada** (Staff Discussion Paper 2025-8, May 2025): the NPR–Canadian-born wage gap **doubled from 9.5% (2006–14) to 22.6% (2023–24)**, and the surge in lower-skilled NPR inflows materially weakened measured Canadian productivity.
- **C.D. Howe Institute (Mikal Skuterud)**: "Canada's Missing Workers" (2023) and "The Growing Data Gap on Canada's Temporary Resident Workforce" (2024) — argues StatsCan's LFS *undercounts* NPRs by ~3–4 pp of the 15+ population, meaning the foreign-born share of jobs is even higher than the headline numbers suggest.
- **Fraser Institute** (Finlayson & Globerman, 2025): "Canada's Changing Immigration Patterns 2000–2024" — annual immigration grew 3.5%/yr (2000–15) then **21.3%/yr (2016–24)**; companion 2025 study finds highly-educated immigrants in Canada have a **9.5% lower employment rate than native-born Canadians**, the inverse of the US (where immigrants do *better* than native-born).
- **Financial Accountability Office of Ontario** (2024 report on immigrant labour-market outcomes): explicitly uses Table 14-10-0083-01; finds immigrants drove 63% of Ontario's 2015–2022 labour-force growth, with NPRs adding more.
- **CBC** (Jan 2024 series, "Unsettling"): documented the surge of NPRs from 1.7M to 2.5M in 12 months and the warnings federal civil servants gave about housing/services.
- **Globe and Mail** ("The good and bad of Canada's immigrant jobs boom," 2023; Mahboubi op-ed 2025 on system reform): document the two-step immigration mechanism and the late-2024 policy reversal.
- **Migration Policy Institute** (2025 country profile): immigrants = **28.9% of Canada's labour force in 2023**, the highest in the G7.

## Details

### What the closest Canadian "9 of 10" comparison looks like

Combining the available StatsCan data points and the C.D. Howe/Bank of Canada adjustments:

- **Total Canadian net job growth, Feb 2020 (pre-COVID peak) to late 2024**: ≈ **+1.7 million**.
- Of that, **roughly 800K–900K went to landed (permanent-resident) immigrants** and **roughly 800K–1,000K went to non-permanent residents** (using IRCC work-permit administrative data, which Skuterud at Waterloo argues is more accurate than the LFS).
- **Canadian-born net employment gain over the same period: roughly zero to +200K**, depending on the exact start/end month and whether you adjust for population aging (the Canadian-born 15+ population is barely growing as boomers retire).
- **Foreign-born share of net new jobs ≈ 90–100%+** — even higher than the US figure of ~90%.
- This is broadly consistent with the StatsCan official statement that **immigration accounted for 79.9% of labour-force growth from 2016–2021** — and the post-2021 NPR surge pushed the post-pandemic figure even higher.

### Why a single, clean Canadian number doesn't exist

1. **Frequency mismatch**: BLS publishes monthly foreign-born / native-born employment in CPS Table A-7. StatsCan's equivalent (14-10-0083-01 / now 14-10-0472-01) is **annual**, with a 3-month moving average version (14-10-0082-01 / 14-10-0471) but not a clean monthly seasonally-adjusted series.
2. **NPR coverage gap**: C.D. Howe's Skuterud has repeatedly shown the LFS sample frame misses ~3–4 pp of the NPR population, so even the official immigrant employment numbers understate the true foreign-born contribution to job growth.
3. **Methodology revisions**: In January 2025, StatsCan revised the LFS series back to 2021 with new population control totals; data from 2006–2024 for "landed immigrants" and "immigrants landed 5 or fewer years earlier" were corrected on January 29, 2025. This makes a clean Feb-2020 → 2025 comparison genuinely unstable.
4. **Two-step immigration**: many people counted as "Canadian-born" gainers were not — they were students/TFWs who transitioned to PR status mid-period and reclassified.

### Pre-COVID peak comparison

For consistency with the US framing (Feb 2020 = pre-COVID peak):
- Feb 2020 Canadian total employment: ~19.4M (LFS, age 15+).
- Dec 2025 Canadian total employment: ~21.0M (StatsCan LFS, Dec 2025 release).
- Net change: **roughly +1.6M jobs**, of which the lion's share is foreign-born.

## Recommendations

**For citing this comparison publicly, here is the most defensible language:**

1. **Best single-line Canadian equivalent**: "From 2016 through 2021, immigrants accounted for **79.9% of Canada's labour-force growth** (Statistics Canada, 2022 Census release). Once non-permanent residents are added, **virtually 100% of Canada's net post-COVID employment growth from 2020 to 2024 went to people born outside Canada**, with the Canadian-born employment level essentially flat and the Canadian-born employment rate falling by 2 percentage points since 2016."

2. **Best directly numeric statement** (using StatsCan annual LFS plus IRCC NPR data):
 - Net Canadian employment growth, 2019 → 2024: **+1.7 million**
 - Foreign-born (landed immigrants + NPRs) share: **~90–100%+**
 - US comparison: 90% / 4.3M of 4.8M

3. **Avoid claiming an exact monthly Feb-2020-to-current decomposition** — Canada's data infrastructure does not support it cleanly. Instead use annual averages or the 79.9% StatsCan figure.

4. **Watch the 2026 reversal**: If you are framing this as "Canada is doing the same thing as the US," note that **Canada is now in the opposite phase** — net NPR outflows of 300K+ in 2025 mean foreign-born employment is *contracting* and the question shifts to whether Canadian-born workers will absorb the unemployment that follows.

5. **Threshold for updating the claim**: If StatsCan's 2025 annual LFS (release Jan 2026) shows immigrant employment falling and Canadian-born employment rising — which RBC, BMO, and CIBC all forecast — the "100% of jobs to foreign-born" framing will be obsolete by mid-2026.

## Caveats

- **No exact Canadian analogue exists** to the BLS CPS Table A-7 monthly foreign-born/native-born employment table indexed to Feb 2020. The numbers above are *constructed* from StatsCan annual tables (14-10-0083-01 / 14-10-0472-01), the StatsCan LFS Daily releases, IRCC administrative permit data, and bank-economist analyses — not from a single official publication.
- **NPR undercount**: StatsCan's LFS underestimates NPRs by an estimated 3–4 percentage points of the 15+ population (C.D. Howe Skuterud 2023, 2024). True foreign-born share of net employment growth is therefore likely *higher* than even the figures quoted above.
- **Methodology breaks**: StatsCan revised the LFS population calibration in January 2025 and again later in 2025 (April 6 – October 11, 2025 temporary measure); also corrected landed-immigrant data on January 29, 2025. Series before and after these breaks are not perfectly comparable.
- **"Canadian-born" in StatsCan ≠ "native-born U.S." in BLS** — notably, StatsCan's "Canadian-born" includes Indigenous people and Canadian-born children of immigrants; BLS "native-born" is broader still. Direct one-to-one comparisons should be treated as approximate.
- **Reverse causation in 2025**: Some of the apparent "Canadian-born resilience" in late-2025 data simply reflects the fact that NPRs were leaving the country (and dropping out of LFS samples), not that Canadian-born were being preferentially hired.
- **Source quality**: Bank of Canada SDP 2025-8, C.D. Howe (Skuterud), and StatsCan analytical articles are the most authoritative; CIC News, Remitbee, ImmigCanada and Statista summaries cited above are secondary aggregators and were used only to corroborate the official numbers.
- **The "+4.3M / +471K / 90%" US figures** in the user's prompt are accepted as given; this report does not independently verify the US BLS CPS A-7 numbers.
