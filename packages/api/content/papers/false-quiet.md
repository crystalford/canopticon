# False Quiet

*Why what works stops being checked. Working paper.*

**Thesis.** People check when something feels wrong: a symptom, a contrast, a guess that missed, a doubt. Each of those signals can be silenced by the response to the problem, or by something that stands in for the check, while the problem stays. From inside, that silence is the same as the problem being gone. Four theories on this site describe four ways it happens. Together they show that the remedy is always the same kind of move: take the reading from somewhere the response cannot reach. McLuhan asked for this in 1965, as a thermostat for the senses and as anti-environments that must be constantly renewed; the four theories show what such a thermostat has to read.

### The claims, stated formally

**Research question.** Self-extinguishing maintenance, ground blindness, cognitive ectothermy and the certainty handoff were each worked out from a different problem: a routine that stops when it works, a tool that cannot be judged while it is in use, a medium that answers before the receiver has predicted, and a conclusion passed on without its grounds. Do they share a mechanism, and if so, what follows from it?

**Answer.** They share one. In each, a person's check runs on a felt discrepancy, and the discrepancy goes quiet for a reason other than the problem being solved. The four differ in how the quiet is produced: the signal is suppressed by the response, adapted away, pre-empted before it can form, or replaced by a stand-in. In each, the check that works takes its reading from somewhere the response cannot reach.

**Contributions.**

- A single mechanism, named here *false quiet*, that joins four theories built separately.
- An account of why false quiet cannot be detected from inside: it is the counterfactual problem of causal inference met in everyday life.
- A classification of the ways a signal is silenced: suppressed, adapted, pre-empted and substituted.
- One rule that generates each theory's remedy: an independent reading.
- McLuhan's anti-environment, stated as that rule: a means of perception the environment cannot reach, renewed at intervals.
- The same four forms found in institutions, where engineering already practises the remedy under other names.

## Abstract

A person checks when something feels wrong. A sore gum prompts cleaning, a mistake in a guess prompts learning, a doubt prompts a second look. When the feeling goes, so does the check, and usually that is correct, because usually the feeling goes when the problem does.

This paper describes the cases where it does not. Four theories on this site, worked out separately, each describe a signal that goes quiet while its cause remains. A routine silences the symptom that prompted it (self-extinguishing maintenance). Steady use removes the contrast that would show what a tool is doing (ground blindness). An answer arrives before the receiver has predicted it, so no error is ever produced (cognitive ectothermy). A feeling of rightness stands in for the check, and the reasons stay with the sender (the certainty handoff).

Read together, they describe one mechanism in four forms: the signal is suppressed, adapted, pre-empted or substituted. From inside, each kind of silence looks the same as resolution, which is the counterfactual problem of causal inference met in everyday life. Each theory's remedy is the same move: take the reading from somewhere the response cannot reach. McLuhan named the problem in 1965, when he wrote that new environments reset our sensory thresholds and asked for the equivalent of a thermostat to measure them; his anti-environment is this remedy. The same four forms appear in vaccination programmes, launch decisions, automated cockpits and computer memory, and in engineering the remedy already has names: proof testing, scrubbing, chaos engineering.

## 1. Four quiet signals

**Each of the four theories begins with a signal that stopped.** In every case the person did the reasonable thing with the information they had.

A gum sore from trapped food prompts careful cleaning. Within a week it looks healthy, the soreness is gone, and the cleaning stops. Two or three weeks later the gum is sore again, and it seems like a new problem. Self-extinguishing maintenance describes this cycle: the routine removes the symptom that prompted it and rewarded it, so the routine ends while the cause, two teeth that trap food, is unchanged.

A phone dies on the way to a friend's house, and the driver finds they do not know the route, after driving it a dozen times. While the navigation app was running there was nothing to notice. Ground blindness describes this: what is in steady use stops being seen, and only its absence shows what it was doing.

A question half forms and an answer arrives from a search box or a chatbot before the person has guessed. Nothing they predicted is corrected, because they predicted nothing, and a week later they could not reproduce the answer. Cognitive ectothermy describes this: a medium that supplies the output first leaves nothing to learn from and leaves the capacity to produce it untrained.

A developer asks an AI agent to rename something because the name is wrong. The reason is obvious to the developer, so it goes unsaid. The agent guesses, builds on the guess, and an hour of work is undone later. The certainty handoff describes this: the conclusion felt right, so it was not checked; the reasons stayed behind, so the receiver could not check it either.

In each story a signal that would normally have prompted a check, the soreness, a sense of what the app was doing, the error in a guess, a doubt about the rename, was missing. In none was it missing because the problem had been solved.

## 2. A check runs on a discrepancy

**In November 1965, McLuhan asked for a thermostat.** Writing on the relation of environment to anti-environment, he said that "the need of our time is for the means of measuring sensory thresholds and of discovering exactly what changes occur in these thresholds as a result of the advent of any particular technology." Such knowledge, he went on, "would be the equivalent of a thermostatic control for room temperatures" [1]. The image is more exact than it looks, and this paper takes it literally.

**Most of what people do to keep things right is a feedback loop.** They compare how things are with how they should be, act when there is a gap, and stop when the gap closes.

Carver and Scheier set out this view of behaviour in 1982: a person perceives the present state, compares it with a reference, and acts to reduce the discrepancy [2]. The same structure appears at every scale the four theories cover. A symptom is a discrepancy between how a body feels and how it should feel, and it prompts and rewards the routine that relieves it [3] [4]. Learning is driven by the gap between what was predicted and what arrived: dopamine neurons in the primate fire for an unexpected reward and fall silent when the reward is fully predicted [5]. Reasoning has its own gate. A first answer comes with a feeling of rightness, and only when that feeling is low do people rethink and change their answers [6].

That is McLuhan's thermostat, and the loop is sound. A thermostat compares the room with its setting, heats when the room is cold and stops when it is warm, and the house stays comfortable for decades.

**It fails in one way that matters here: when the sensor reads something the response controls instead of the thing it is meant to watch.** A thermostat mounted beside a radiator reads warm as soon as the radiator heats, and shuts it off while the rest of the room stays cold. The loop works perfectly, and the room is still cold.

Each of the four theories describes a person whose sensor has been placed beside the radiator. The symptom reads the routine instead of the cause. The sense of how things are reads a baseline that has moved. The learning signal never fires because nothing was predicted. The feeling of rightness reads fluency instead of validity.

## 3. Two silences that look the same

**The quiet produced by a working response and the quiet produced by a solved problem are the same experience.** Nothing available from inside tells them apart.

This is a familiar problem under another name. Holland called it the fundamental problem of causal inference: a unit cannot be observed both with and without a treatment at the same time, so the effect of the treatment on that unit is never seen directly [7]. A person keeping up a routine sees only the state with the routine. As the research behind self-extinguishing maintenance puts it, while the routine runs, the person never sees what would happen without it, so the healthy state is credited to the body healing rather than to the routine holding it.

The other three forms meet the same problem from different sides. A tool in steady use is judged only by what it is like to have it. A person who received an answer before guessing never learns what their own answer would have been. A receiver handed a conclusion has only the conclusion, never the case it was drawn from.

Research methods built to solve this problem show what an answer looks like. Clinical trials test long-term treatments by withdrawing them from patients who responded and watching who relapses [8]. Single-case designs in behaviour analysis establish that a treatment works by withdrawing it and restoring it [9]. Both produce the missing observation on purpose. Neither relies on the quiet.

**False quiet is therefore not a failure of attention or of character.** A person attending perfectly to the signal they have will still read a held quiet as a solved problem, because on the evidence available the two are identical.

## 4. Four ways a signal goes quiet

**The theories differ in how the quiet is produced.** Four mechanisms account for all of them.

| Form | The signal | What quiets it | What remains | Theory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Suppressed | A symptom | The routine holds it down | The cause | Self-extinguishing maintenance |
| Adapted | The contrast between having and not having | Habituation and a moved baseline | The dependence and its costs | Ground blindness |
| Pre-empted | The error between a guess and an answer | The answer arrives first | An untrained capacity | Cognitive ectothermy |
| Substituted | A doubt, or a question from the receiver | A feeling of rightness, and grounds left behind | The unchecked error | The certainty handoff |

**Suppressed.** The response acts directly on the signal. A routine that relieves a symptom also removes the reminder to act and the reward for acting. The pattern is measured across very different conditions. Across surveys in 13 countries, feeling better was the most common reason people gave for stopping antidepressants (46.6%) [10]. About half of patients prescribed a blood-pressure drug had stopped within a year [11]. Where the cause persists, the symptom returns on a measurable schedule: skin dryness reverted to baseline six days after moisturiser stopped [12], and gums developed gingivitis within about 9–21 days without cleaning [13].

**Adapted.** The signal is never suppressed; the person stops registering it. A response to a steady, unchanging stimulus declines, which is habituation [14]. A body or a habit then adjusts its baseline to what is always there. In habitual caffeine users, much of what feels like a boost is the reversal of withdrawal [15]. McLuhan put the same point in an image he credited to Bertrand Russell: "if the bath water got only half a degree warmer every hour, we would never know when to scream." His gloss is the mechanism: "New environments reset our sensory thresholds" [1]. The contrast that would show what the thing is doing is now available only by taking it away, or by replacing it: "When an environment is new, we perceive the old one for the first time" [1]. After four weeks without Facebook, people valued it less than before, which suggests that the value people state while using a service overstates it [16].

**Pre-empted.** The signal would have been an error, and no error can form without a prediction. When the answer comes first there is nothing to correct. Students who practised maths with an AI that gave answers improved their practice grades by 48% and then scored 17% below students who never had it once it was taken away; students given an AI that offered hints instead did not suffer that loss [17]. In laboratory work, trying to answer before studying, even unsuccessfully, improved later recall [18]. The quiet here is also felt. Students taught by active methods learned more than students given a polished lecture, but felt they had learned less [19]: the feeling of learning was highest where the learning signal was weakest.

**Substituted.** The comparison is replaced by something that feels like its result. The feeling of rightness tracks how fluently an answer came rather than whether it is valid [6], and it can attach to wrong answers: 37% of incorrect solutions to magic tricks were rated as stronger insights than the average correct one [20]. When the conclusion is passed on, the sender expects to be understood more often than they are [21], and the receiver has no grounds to test it. Language models, the newest receivers, seldom ask what an instruction meant [22]. Substitution also runs in reverse. People overestimated the accuracy of model answers given with explanations, and longer explanations raised their confidence without raising accuracy [23]; programmers with an AI assistant wrote less secure code and were more likely to believe it secure [24].

**The forms combine.** A navigation app adapts its user, who stops noticing it, and pre-empts them, since the turn is announced before they would have recalled it. A chatbot pre-empts the question it answers and substitutes its fluency for the reader's check. The worst cases in the four theories are often two forms at once, which is part of why the four theories kept running into each other while they were being written.

## 5. Taking the reading from elsewhere

**Each theory arrived at its own remedy. All four are the same rule: replace a signal the response controls with a reading it does not.**

| Form | The signal the response controls | The independent reading | Where it is tested |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Suppressed | The symptom | A daily habit the symptom does not affect, and a direct look at the cause | Proactive treatment of cleared eczema reduced flares in randomised trials [25] |
| Adapted | The felt sense of having it | A brief, planned removal | Discontinuation trials [8]; withdrawal designs [9]; chaos engineering [26] |
| Pre-empted | The arrival of the answer | A prediction made before the answer | Pretesting [18]; problem-solving before instruction [27] |
| Substituted | The feeling of rightness | A check the other side can run, and the reason stated | Explaining step by step lowers unearned confidence [28]; programmers who trusted the assistant less wrote more secure code [24] |

**Suppressed.** If the symptom is held down by the routine, it can no longer serve as the cue. The routine is tied to something the routine does not affect, such as a meal, a shower or getting dressed, and the cause is checked directly. In eczema, scheduled treatment of skin that had already cleared reduced flares against no treatment, with risk ratios of 0.46 and 0.78 [25]. Testing by stopping is the wrong move here: it costs a relapse to learn what was already known.

**Adapted.** If the baseline has moved, the only clean comparison is a period without the thing. It should be brief, planned and reversible, and long enough to pass any withdrawal, which for caffeine lasts two to nine days [29].

**Pre-empted.** If the answer arrives too soon to produce an error, the person produces one: a guess, a recall, a sketch of the solution, before looking. The answer then corrects something the person made.

**Substituted.** If a feeling stands in for the check, the check has to be one the feeling cannot satisfy: a test that fails when the conclusion is wrong, an example of the case that prompted it, or an explanation written out step by step. People rate their understanding of how devices work lower after writing out each step [28].

**This is McLuhan's anti-environment, stated as a rule.** He wrote that "the artist provides us with anti-environments that enable us to see the environment," and added a condition: "Such anti-environmental means of perception must constantly be renewed in order to be efficacious" [1]. An independent reading is an anti-environment for one signal. The condition holds too. A removal, a guess or a test taken once becomes part of the environment in turn, which is why the engineering versions in section 6 run on a schedule.

**The remedies share a cost and a benefit.** Each makes work that the quiet had spared: a routine continued on good days, a week without coffee, a wrong guess, a test written for something obvious. That cost is what makes the quiet attractive. The benefit is a reading that means what it appears to mean.

## 6. The same forms in institutions

**Organisations and machines show all four forms, and their remedies have been invented independently.**

Suppressed. Vaccination suppresses the disease it targets, and as the disease becomes rare, the vaccine's side effects become more visible than the illness it prevents; a model long used in vaccine safety work traces how confidence then falls, coverage drops and outbreaks return [30]. A review of the obstacles to prevention lists first that "the success of prevention is invisible" [31].

Adapted. Before the Challenger disaster, a run of successful launches with eroded seals shifted what engineers and managers believed about the odds; Starbuck and Milliken describe how past successes and acclimatisation altered decision-makers' beliefs about the probability of future success, and how fine-tuning on that basis reduced the margins until something broke [32]. Rasmussen described the same drift as a migration of work practices toward the boundary of safe performance, under steady pressure for efficiency [33].

Pre-empted. When automation flies the aircraft or runs the plant, the operator stops forming their own picture of the process, and is left unpractised for the moment the automation fails, which Bainbridge called an irony of automation [34].

Substituted. People working with automated aids accept the machine's output without checking it, and miss what the aid does not flag; a review brought complacency and automation bias together as a single attentional failure [35].

**Engineering already practises the independent reading for machines.** Error-correcting memory quietly fixes single-bit errors, which means a second error in the same word can accumulate unseen; memory scrubbing reads every location on a schedule to catch the first error before the second arrives [36]. Safety systems that act only in an emergency are proof-tested at intervals, because their failures stay hidden until the emergency. Reason described such dormant flaws in organisations as latent conditions, which "may lie dormant within the system for many years" and "can be identified and remedied before an adverse event occurs" [37]. Chaos engineering takes production servers down on purpose to find the dependencies that only a failure would reveal [26].

None of these practices waits for a signal. Each assumes that a quiet system may be holding a fault and goes to look.

## 7. Why the lesson comes late

**False quiet is learned slowly, and usually more than once.** Two features of the situation make it hard to learn from.

The first is delay. People judge one thing to cause another far less readily when time separates them, and less of the difficulty remains when they know how long a delay to expect [38]. A relapse that comes two weeks after a routine stopped, a skill that fails a year after the app took it over, a bug that surfaces after a week of work built on it: each arrives long after the decision that caused it, and often looks like a new problem.

The second is the shape of the costs. The cost of keeping up the check is paid every day and is easy to see. The cost of stopping is paid later, at an unknown time, and was last paid long enough ago to be forgotten. A person weighing the two at the moment of deciding sees only one. The same asymmetry runs through prevention as a whole: its success is invisible, it lacks drama, and its benefits may be long delayed [31].

The feelings that accompany false quiet also point the wrong way. Relief accompanies the suppressed symptom. Comfort accompanies the adapted baseline. Fluency accompanies the pre-empted answer, and the students taught most effectively felt they had learned least [19]. Confidence accompanies the substituted check. In each form, the moment the check is most needed is a moment that feels like it needs no check at all.

## 8. One question

**Every case in this paper reduces to one question: is it quiet because the problem has gone, or because something is keeping it quiet?**

Four narrower questions, one for each form, make it usable:

1. **Would the signal come back if I stopped?** If the routine is what keeps it away, the quiet is suppressed. Tie the routine to something you already do every day, and look at the cause.
2. **When did I last go without it?** If the answer is months, the quiet may be adapted. A short, planned removal will show what it is doing.
3. **Did I make my own answer before this one arrived?** If not, the quiet is pre-empted. Guess first next time.
4. **Does my confidence rest on a check, or on how it feels?** If on how it feels, the quiet is substituted. Write the reason, and give the other side something to run.

This is a working tool assembled from the four theories' own checks. It has not been validated as a set.

## 9. Objections

**"This is 'absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.'"** The maxim warns against reading too much into silence. It does not say where the silence comes from, which forms it takes or what to do about each. The claim here is narrower and more useful: the silence is produced by the response itself, in one of four ways, and each way has a matching reading.

**"Most quiet is real quiet."** It is. A cold heals, a lesson sticks, a hunch is right more often than not. The mechanism matters only where the cause is standing, the response is ongoing, or someone else will build on the result. The four questions are meant for those cases, not for every quiet moment.

**"Testing what works can do harm."** It can. Stopping a prescribed medicine to see what happens, or switching off a safety system to check it, can cost more than it reveals. That is why the remedy is an independent reading and not a removal. For a suppressed signal, the reading is a direct look at the cause; removal is the remedy only where the thing is easily restored and its absence costs little.

**"Four theories by one author have been fitted to one idea."** The risk is real, and the classification was found after the theories were written. Two things count against a forced fit. The four forms work by different means, through the response, the baseline, the order of events and a stand-in, and do not collapse into one another. And the same forms turn up, with remedies invented independently, in fields the theories did not draw on: vaccine safety, launch decisions, reliability engineering.

**"McLuhan already said this."** He said the most important part of it, and this paper borrows his thermostat and his anti-environment. He also wrote that the condition "by which we are rendered incapable of perceiving the environment is one to which psychologists have not even referred" [1]. Psychologists have since measured each piece: habituation, adherence, pretesting, the feeling of rightness. What the four theories add is the split into four mechanisms, each with its evidence, and a reading for each that a person can take.

**"Control theory already says all this."** Control theory supplies the loop and the misplaced sensor. It does not say which human signals are misplaced, in which ways, or what an everyday independent reading looks like for each. That is the work of the four theories, and this paper is what they have in common.

## 10. Limits

**The synthesis is argued.** No study has tested the four forms as one mechanism or the independent-reading rule across them. The propositions are graded accordingly.

**The classification may not be complete.** Other ways for a signal to go quiet may exist, for instance a signal that is noticed but deliberately ignored. Alarm fatigue, where clinicians exposed to many false alarms stop responding to true ones, is a neighbouring failure in the other direction: too much signal rather than too little [39].

**The evidence is uneven across the forms.** Pre-emption and substitution rest on experiments, several of them replicated. Suppression rests mostly on surveys and observational studies of adherence. Adaptation rests on habituation research, a small number of removal experiments and work on dependence.

**The institutional cases are illustrations.** The vaccine model, the Challenger analysis and the drift account are case studies and reviews. They show that the forms recur; they do not measure how often.

**The remedies are tested separately.** Each is supported in its own field. That an independent reading reduces false quiet in general follows from the argument and has not been studied directly.
