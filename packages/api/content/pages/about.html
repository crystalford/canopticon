About this site

An independent research publication by Chris Telford: theories, the essays and papers that argue them, and the research they stand on.

1Who writes this

Chris Telford commissions deep research on whatever question he is working through — science, media, technology, the products he is building — and keeps every document whole here.

He writes from it, in four sections: theories that state an idea formally; writing, the essays and papers that argue it; research, the documents themselves and the briefs written from them; and a lexicon of names for patterns nobody had named.

2What you will find

Theory
Theories
A named idea stated formally: the claim, how it works, propositions graded by their evidence, how to measure it, what to do about it, where it applies and what counts against it. Each is versioned and citable.
Essay
Essays
Writing. Short arguments for one of the author’s ideas: each names a pattern or applies one of the theories.
Paper
Papers
Writing. Long original arguments that cite the published literature, with numbered references: a seven-paper series on McLuhan and the senses, and the papers that build on it.
Research
Research
Research. The documents themselves, kept whole: deep-research reports, evidence reviews, project briefs, and the investigations on Canadian politics, finance and media.
Brief
Briefs
Research. Short answers to one question, written from the reports.
Term
The Lexicon
Patterns the work found that had no name, so they were given one, after checking nobody already had. Each term has its definition, pronunciation and examples.
Method
Methods
Procedures built from the theories, to run on any piece of writing: the steps for a person, the exact instruction for a language model, a worked example and where each fails. Each downloads as a skill for Claude.
Open question
Open questions
Questions nobody has answered, or things nobody has built, each linked to the document that raised it.

3For machines

For readers who build tools: every page is plain HTML, and each document also comes as Markdown and JSON.

/p/<slug>The page. Complete HTML, no JavaScript required to read it. Article and Person markup with the author's sameAs; a Dataset block describing the key-figures table; every named source as a citation. FAQ markup is not emitted — Google retired FAQ rich results for most sites in 2023, so the question-and-answer shape is carried in headings and the answering sentence instead. Indexed. /p/<slug>.mdThe document, byte-exact, with its front matter. For agents and for you. Not indexed; canonical is the page. /p/<slug>.jsonEverything found in the document, typed, with receipts. Not indexed. /q/<id>A receipt: the passage, its location, its check. Not indexed; canonical is the document at the anchor. /llms.txt · /llms-full.txtOne line per document, grouped by project, in the document's own words. Kept because it is cheap; its fetches are counted rather than assumed. /sitemap.xml · /feed.jsonDocuments and articles with the date they were last read. A new version pings IndexNow. citation_* metaHighwire tags on every theory, paper and article — title, author, date, the DOI where there is one — so Google Scholar indexes them as scholarly work. Research documents carry none: Scholar is for the writing, not the commissioned reports. #:~:text=Every receipt is a text-fragment link that highlights its passage. Deep links that survive re-reads, because they are made of the words. /searchPlain full-text search over passages and documents. Not indexed. AI crawlers are allowed at the edge and the allowance is checked weekly.
