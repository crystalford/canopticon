Self-extinguishing maintenance

DefinitionApproved by Chris Telford

A routine that works so well it removes the thing that made you do it. The symptom was both the reminder and the reward; once the routine clears it, the routine stops, and because the cause was never the symptom but something that doesn't heal — the shape of a toe, a gap between two teeth — the problem comes back.

2The pieces that already exist

The belief — the acute model of a chronic condition53% of 198 inner-city adults hospitalized for asthma "believed they only had asthma when they were having symptoms." Halm, Mora & Leventhal 2006 · Key finding 1
The behaviour — intentional non-adherenceWHO surveys across 13 countries: "the main patient-reported reason for discontinuation was feeling better (46.6%)" Kazdin et al. 2024 · Key finding 2
The engine — relief removes the motiveWhen the aversive state behind a negatively reinforced behaviour is gone, the person "would no longer be motivated to engage in the behavior." B.1
The evidence it costs youStopping supportive periodontal therapy: "non-compliers in SPT have an increased risk of tooth loss when compared with compliers." Campos et al. 2021 · Section D

3Why it's new

  1. No cross-domain name or model for the loop.
  2. No framing of "success removes the motive" for self-care, even though it follows from established principles.
  3. No work on structural or anatomical causes as the defining feature of "maintenance forever" conditions.
From the research, Section F. The research is explicit about its own framing: They are consistent with established principles but not tested as such.

4How to tell it apart

Is the cause still there? Transient causes heal. Anatomy, spacing and genetics don't — they need maintenance until the structure changes.
Count the relapses. Two at the same site with the same trigger is evidence of a standing cause.
Take the cue off the symptom. Tie the routine to a fixed moment — after the shower, after each meal — so it doesn't disappear when it works.
Name the exit. The structural fix that would end the routine: restoring the contact, changing the footwear. Removal beats maintenance.
From the research's recommendations. Not medical advice; persistent changes deserve an in-person look.

5What would test it

A study of symptom-cued self-care decaying after remission — I did not find a study of symptom-cued self-care decaying after remission.
Whether people who learn it once apply it elsewhere — No research on learning the general lesson, and nothing on age at insight.
