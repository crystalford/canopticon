A moisturiser works, your skin feels fine, and you stop using it. A drink takes the edge off, and over years the edge gets sharper. A price on a screen reads the same as the price on a tag. In each case something changed, and the thing that changed was not the thing you were looking at. This article follows that pattern through five pieces of research that were never meant to be read together.

## A routine that works erases its own reminder

The first case is the simplest. Some problems only stay fixed while you keep treating them, and the treatment, by working, removes the signal that kept you going. The research named it <q doc="when-healed-is-the-treatment-working-the-stopping-mechanism-in-mainten" section="§G">a maintenance behavior that, by succeeding, removes the symptom that cues and reinforces it, so that the successful state is misread as cure and the behavior stops, while an unchanged underlying cause guarantees relapse.</q> The skin looks healthy. What has changed is the condition underneath, held in place by a routine you no longer notice doing.

## In addiction, the baseline moves, not the drug

Addiction looks like the opposite case — the drug is all a person notices. The research makes the distinction that resolves it: <q doc="the-medium-becomes-the-ground-mcluhan-s-media-theory-and-the-allostati" section="TL;DR">In the allostatic model, the thing that becomes the unperceived "ground" is not the drug. It is the drug's opponent process: the anti-reward, stress-surfeit baseline. The drug stays a hyper-salient figure.</q> The craving is in plain view. The shifted normal it answers to is not. McLuhan, in 1964, put it in four words: <q doc="the-medium-becomes-the-ground-mcluhan-s-media-theory-and-the-allostati" section="Part 2">Self-amputation forbids self-recognition.</q>

## A price can change meaning without changing number

Money shows the same shape. <q doc="the-price-frontier-from-discovery-to-administration-a-canopticon-resea" section="§5">A price discovered through decentralized markets is a different *medium* than a price computed by an algorithm and pushed at you — even when the number is identical.</q> Nothing on the screen tells you which kind you are looking at, and that is the point: <q doc="the-price-frontier-from-discovery-to-administration-a-canopticon-resea" section="§5">The shift from discovery to administration is a medium-change that the "it's just a price" framing renders invisible.</q>

## A habit forms where you are not watching

Scrolling feels like a series of choices. The best study of it found the choices were the visible part, and the habit forming underneath was not: people are <q doc="diagnosing-the-feed-why-scrolling-works-why-x-feels-like-a-town-square" section="§1">"inattentive to habit formation,"</q> <q doc="diagnosing-the-feed-why-scrolling-works-why-x-feels-like-a-town-square" section="§1">meaning they underestimate how much today's scrolling shapes tomorrow's</q>.

## A whole era can feel normal

The pattern also works at the scale of a culture. For thirty years, talking to computers meant typing to them, and it felt like the natural state of things. The research suggests it was a detour: <q doc="acoustic-return-research-pass-mcluhan-voice-interfaces-fifty-year" section="Genuinely untheorized">the internet from roughly 1990 to 2022 was an anomalous typographic intensification *within* what McLuhan correctly predicted as an acoustic-restoration trajectory.</q> Only now that voice is returning does the keyboard era look like an era.

> **Objection: This is just McLuhan’s figure and ground**
>
> Yes — and the name is his. What the record adds is the range. The same mechanism turns up in a skin routine, a nervous system, a price, a habit and a medium, in research that was never looking for McLuhan. A frame that keeps appearing where it was not invited is doing work, not decoration.

## Seeing the background takes work

If the change hides in the background, noticing it is a skill, not an accident. One of the record's reviews of how thinking watches itself frames it this way: <q doc="examined-cognition-substrates-metacognition-methods-looking-looking" section="Key Findings">metacognition is the labor of dragging ground into figure</q>. Every case above has the same remedy: make the background visible on purpose. Keep the routine after the symptom is gone. Name the baseline, not the craving. Ask how a price was set, not just what it is. Count the hours, not the swipes. Notice the keyboard.

## What this explains

It explains why so many problems come back after they look solved: what fixed them was a background condition, and nobody was watching it. It explains why the most powerful changes are the quiet ones — a price that means something new, a habit that becomes a default, a medium that becomes the room. And it explains why the research kept finding the same shape: not because one idea explains everything, but because people attend to what is in front of them, and the world changes behind it.
