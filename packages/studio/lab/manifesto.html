Deterministic editorial compiler

A film is a compiled artifact.

Canopticon treats documentary as source code: a versioned narrative representation, a creative state that evolves across the runtime, and a render graph that only recomputes what actually changed. Edit one sentence and the film rebuilds around it in seconds.

The premise

Code is a medium, not an assembly mechanism.

Most tools in this category generate a shot and hope it fits. Canopticon composes a passage from typed primitives — geometry, fields, particles, documents, data, type — and every one of them declares what it consumes and what it produces.

Because glyph outlines output geometry, and a particle system accepts geometry as targets, text becomes particles without either primitive knowing the other exists. That single property is what separates a palette from a template library.

The director never names a renderer. They set a trajectory — grounded here, analytical through the middle, abstract at the turn — and the composition engine selects primitives whose parameters express it, inside the rules and invariants they've declared.

Blocks still exist. They're recipes: convenient defaults the engine can reach past when the passage deserves better.

NOT A VIDEO GENERATORNo model is called at render time. TTS audio and alignment are generated once, frozen, hashed, and committed as input artifacts.
NOT A TEMPLATE ENGINEThere are no four looks in rotation. Primitives chain, and the chain is authored by creative state rather than picked from a menu.
NOT AN AI DIRECTORThe system proposes; the operator ratifies. Nothing mutates the source representation without a human saying so.
The compile loop

Change one sentence. Watch what doesn't move.

The constraint solver re-anchors timing against the narration; only primitives whose inputs actually changed recompute. Everything else is a content-addressed cache hit, and the Compile view states the reason for every recomputation in plain language.

Pinned decisions survive restyling. If a global change would contradict a pin, the system honours the pin and reports the tension rather than resolving it unilaterally. That is the difference between an instrument and an automaton.

The instrument

Direction is a curve, not a setting.

Creative state is a continuous vector — abstraction, archival weight, data weight, density, human presence, motion energy — defined at control points and interpolated across the runtime. Vocabulary mix is derived from it, not authored.

Invariants hold across everything: a floor under human presence, a ceiling on anything that would make the film feel like a corporate explainer. Drag a curve and the film changes character while its motifs develop rather than reset.
